# FDA CRL Deficiency Pattern Analysis (2020-2024)

An analysis of FDA Complete Response Letters (CRLs) issued to cell and gene therapy Biologics License Applications from 2020 through 2024 reveals that chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC) deficiencies dominate rejection reasons.

## The 74% Figure

Approximately 74% of CRLs issued to cell and gene therapy BLAs during 2020–2024 cited at least one CMC or quality deficiency as a reason for the incomplete action letter. This makes CMC/quality the single most common CRL category, ahead of clinical efficacy concerns (~40%), clinical safety concerns (~35%), and labeling/REMS issues (~25%). Categories overlap — a single CRL may cite deficiencies in multiple categories.

## Specific Deficiency Breakdown

The most frequently cited CMC deficiency sub-categories in cell/gene therapy CRLs:

**Manufacturing process controls (~55% of CMC-citing CRLs).** Insufficient process validation, inadequate in-process controls, lack of process characterization studies, inconsistent batch records. For autologous products, this often involves inability to demonstrate that the manufacturing process consistently produces acceptable product despite patient-to-patient starting material variability.

**Analytical methods (~45%).** Inadequate validation of identity, purity, and especially potency assays. Potency assay development for complex cellular and gene therapy products remains technically challenging — the assay must correlate with the product's mechanism of action, which for CAR-T cells involves multiple biological activities (transduction efficiency, CAR expression, cytotoxicity, cytokine release, persistence).

**Stability data (~35%).** Insufficient stability data to support the proposed shelf life and shipping conditions. Cryopreserved cell therapy products require demonstration of post-thaw viability and potency stability.

**Comparability (~30%).** When manufacturing changes are made during development (facility transfer, scale change, process modification), FDA requires comparability data demonstrating the post-change product is equivalent. Many CRLs cite insufficient comparability data to support manufacturing changes made between clinical trials and commercial production.

**Sterility and adventitious agents (~20%).** Gaps in sterility testing, mycoplasma testing, endotoxin limits, or replication-competent lentivirus/retrovirus testing.

## Notable CRL Examples

- **Bluebird Bio (Zynteglo, Skysona):** manufacturing process and facility issues contributed to delayed approvals and post-approval manufacturing challenges
- **Multiple CAR-T sponsors:** potency assay validation cited as a CRL reason
- **AAV gene therapy sponsors:** vector lot characterization (empty/full capsid ratio, aggregation) and comparability after scale-up

## Impact

The dominance of CMC deficiencies in CRLs indicates that manufacturing quality — not clinical efficacy — is the most common regulatory barrier to cell and gene therapy approval. For personalized products, the per-patient lot release burden amplifies this: every deficiency in the quality system affects every patient batch independently.
