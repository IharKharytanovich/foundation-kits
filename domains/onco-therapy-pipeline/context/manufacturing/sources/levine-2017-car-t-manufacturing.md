# Global Manufacturing of CAR T Cell Therapy

Levine et al. 2017 provided the first comprehensive characterization of the autologous CAR-T manufacturing process, establishing the baseline complexity figures that remain relevant as of 2026.

## Key Findings

**Process complexity.** The end-to-end autologous CAR-T manufacturing workflow involves approximately 50 discrete process steps from leukapheresis to final product release, executed in a GMP cleanroom environment. Each step requires documentation, in-process controls, and chain-of-identity tracking for the individual patient's cells.

**Manufacturing duration.** The cell culture and expansion phase requires approximately 9–12 days for most CAR-T products (e.g., tisagenlecleucel, axicabtagene ciloleucel). Total hands-on processing time across all steps is approximately 80 hours per patient batch, though this is distributed over the multi-day culture period. Vein-to-vein time (from apheresis to patient infusion) is typically 3–5 weeks, including shipping, QC/release testing, and scheduling.

**Lentiviral vector dependency.** Most approved CAR-T products use lentiviral vectors for T cell transduction. Establishing a stable lentiviral vector producer cell line requires 6–12 months and represents a major time and cost bottleneck in clinical development. Transient transfection-based vector production (HEK293T) is used for clinical-scale but has inherent lot-to-lot variability.

**Manufacturing failure.** Autologous manufacturing carries an inherent failure risk of approximately 1–5% — cases where the patient's apheresed T cells fail to expand adequately or meet release specifications. For heavily pretreated patients (multiple prior lines of chemotherapy), failure rates can exceed 10%.

**Cost structure.** The manufacturing cost of goods (COGs) for autologous CAR-T is estimated at $50,000–$100,000 per patient batch, driven by GMP facility time, skilled personnel, consumables, lentiviral vector, and per-patient QC/release testing. List prices for approved CAR-T products ($373,000–$475,000 in the US) reflect these costs plus clinical development amortization.

## Impact

This paper established the ~50-step / ~80-hour characterization that defines the manufacturing complexity baseline. The manual, patient-specific nature of the process — each batch is an individual product — is what makes autologous CAR-T fundamentally different from conventional biologic manufacturing and drives the search for automated and allogeneic alternatives.
