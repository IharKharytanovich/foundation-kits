# Automated and Closed Cell Therapy Manufacturing Systems

The manual nature of conventional CAR-T manufacturing (~50 steps, ~80 hours hands-on) has driven development of automated, closed manufacturing platforms aimed at reducing labor, contamination risk, and facility requirements.

## Major Platforms (2024-2025 State)

**Miltenyi CliniMACS Prodigy.** A closed, automated cell processing system that integrates cell selection, activation, transduction, expansion, washing, and formulation in a single-use tubing set. Reduces open manipulations from ~50 to <5. Processing time for CAR-T manufacturing: 10–14 days (comparable to manual). Installed base >500 units globally as of 2024. Demonstrated in multiple clinical trials (e.g., Novartis academic manufacturing programs).

**Lonza Cocoon.** A closed, automated cell therapy manufacturing platform with individual patient bioreactors. Each unit processes one patient batch, enabling parallel production. The Cocoon automates cell culture, media exchange, and harvest. Designed for decentralized manufacturing (point-of-care or regional manufacturing). Multiple active clinical programs as of 2025.

**Ori Biotech Ori Platform (now part of the broader CDMO landscape).** Focused on automating the cell culture and expansion phase with real-time process monitoring. Aims to reduce manual interventions and enable consistent manufacturing across sites.

## Cost Impact of Automation

Automated manufacturing is projected to reduce COGs by 30–50% through:
- **Labor reduction:** from ~80 hours hands-on to <20 hours operator time
- **Facility simplification:** closed systems reduce cleanroom classification requirements (potential shift from Grade B to Grade C/D)
- **Contamination reduction:** fewer open manipulations reduces batch failure from contamination events
- **Throughput:** parallel processing of multiple patient batches on independent units

Projected automated COGs: $30,000–$60,000 per patient batch (down from $50,000–$100,000 manual).

## Decentralized / Point-of-Care Manufacturing

Automated closed systems enable a decentralized model where CAR-T manufacturing occurs at or near the treatment site, eliminating cryopreservation and shipping logistics. This reduces vein-to-vein time from 3–5 weeks to potentially 10–14 days. Active clinical evaluation by several academic centers as of 2025.

## Limitations

Automation does not solve the fundamental N-of-1 constraint — each patient still requires an individual manufacturing run. It reduces per-batch cost and increases per-site throughput, but total manufacturing capacity still scales linearly with patient volume, unlike mRNA/LNP cell-free manufacturing.

## Impact

Automated platforms represent an evolutionary improvement in cell therapy manufacturing — reducing cost and variability without changing the underlying autologous, per-patient paradigm. The structural N-of-1 constraint remains.
