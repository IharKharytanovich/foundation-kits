# FDA CMC Guidance for Cell and Gene Therapy

FDA's Office of Tissues and Advanced Therapies (OTAT, formerly OCTGT) has issued multiple guidance documents governing the CMC requirements for cell and gene therapy INDs and BLAs.

## Key Guidance Documents

**CMC Information for Human Gene Therapy INDs (2020, revised 2024).** This guidance specifies manufacturing process description, characterization, and control requirements for gene therapy vectors (AAV, lentiviral, retroviral, adenoviral, plasmid DNA). Key requirements include: (1) detailed manufacturing process flow with in-process controls at each step; (2) characterization of the producer cell substrate; (3) qualification of raw materials and reagents; (4) analytical methods for identity, purity, potency, and safety; (5) stability data supporting the proposed shelf life; (6) comparability assessments when manufacturing changes are made.

**Potency assurance.** For autologous products, FDA requires a potency assay that correlates with clinical activity — a requirement that has proven particularly challenging for CAR-T products where the mechanism of action involves complex in-vivo dynamics. Many BLA submissions have received Complete Response Letters (CRLs) citing inadequate potency assay validation.

**Individualized product challenges.** Each patient's product is a distinct lot. The guidance acknowledges that traditional statistical process control (lot-to-lot consistency across many batches) does not apply — instead, the manufacturing process itself must be validated to consistently produce product meeting specifications regardless of starting material variability (patient-to-patient variation in T cell fitness, viral load, prior treatments).

**Chain of identity.** Autologous products require unbroken chain-of-identity documentation from apheresis through manufacturing to infusion. Mix-ups constitute a serious safety risk (infusing another patient's modified cells). Electronic tracking systems, barcode verification at each handoff, and segregation protocols are required.

## CRL Deficiency Patterns

FDA's review of Complete Response Letters for biologics (including cell and gene therapy products) from 2020–2024 shows that chemistry/manufacturing/controls (CMC) deficiencies are the single most common category cited, appearing in approximately 70–75% of CRLs for this product class. Specific deficiency categories include: inadequate manufacturing process validation, insufficient analytical method validation, stability data gaps, comparability data insufficient to support manufacturing changes, and sterility/endotoxin testing inadequacies.

## Impact

The regulatory framework creates a per-patient quality burden that scales linearly with patient volume — unlike conventional biologics where one validated batch serves many patients. This is a structural contributor to the >$100,000 per patient manufacturing cost.
