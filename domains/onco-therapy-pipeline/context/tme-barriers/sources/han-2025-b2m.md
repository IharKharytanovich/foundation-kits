# Han 2025 — β2-Microglobulin Loss and Immunotherapy Resistance

A review quantifying loss of antigen presentation as an immune-escape mechanism, including the frequencies and the important MSI-high colorectal paradox.

## Scope

Review of β2-microglobulin (B2M) function, its loss as a resistance mechanism to checkpoint and cellular immunotherapy, and reversal strategies.

## Quantitative Results

- **Advanced melanoma: 29.4%** carry a B2M mutation, deletion, or loss-of-heterozygosity; B2M loss-of-heterozygosity is **3× more frequent in anti-PD-1 non-responders** than responders.
- **NSCLC: HLA loss-of-heterozygosity in 40%.**
- **MSI-high colorectal: B2M mutations in ~24%** (usually with loss of B2M protein) — yet MSI-high colorectal still benefits from checkpoint inhibitors (~85% derive benefit; a 35-patient cohort found no significant PD-1-efficacy difference by B2M status, p=0.53).
- Prognostic direction: higher B2M expression → better checkpoint-inhibitor response, longer PFS/OS; B2M frameshift mutation → acquired PD-1 resistance.

## The Paradox & Limitations

Frequencies vary by assay (whole-exome sequencing vs immunohistochemistry vs loss-of-heterozygosity) and by cancer. The MSI-high colorectal data show MHC-I loss is **not always an absolute resistance mechanism**: NK cells provide a backup that recognizes MHC-I-low cells, and antigen can be re-presented. So antigen-presentation loss is a strong but context-dependent blocker.

## Conclusion

Loss of MHC-I / β2-microglobulin is a frequent immune-escape mechanism — a quarter to nearly a third of melanoma and NSCLC — that defeats therapies dependent on classical antigen presentation. But its non-absolute nature (NK backup) is a genuine biological nuance: durable control needs effectors that do not depend on a single antigen or on intact MHC-I, reinforcing the case for multi-antigen and innate-effector strategies.
