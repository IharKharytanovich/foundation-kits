# Masugi 2022 — The Desmoplastic Stroma of Pancreatic Cancer

A quantitative review of the physical stromal barrier that makes pancreatic cancer the archetype of immune exclusion and delivery failure.

## Scope & Methods

Narrative review integrating multiple cited primary cohorts, single-cell CAF transcriptomics, and spatial CD8 quantification, focused on pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

## Quantitative Results

- Carcinoma-cell fraction of the tumor bed is **often <20%** → the desmoplastic stroma occupies **>80%** of tumor volume (the basis for the widely cited "up to ~90% stroma").
- **~90% of extracellular-matrix protein is collagen**, and **~90% of that is type I and type III fibrillar collagen**.
- In a cohort of **215 resected pancreatic cancers**: mean collagen occupancy **38.4%**, mean fibroblast (ACTA2⁺ and/or FAP⁺) occupancy **33.3%**.
- Interstitial fluid pressure in the KPC genetically engineered mouse model is **~10× that of healthy pancreas**.
- **T-cell exclusion:** mean CD8 T-cell density in the tumor center is **less than half** that at the tumor margin.
- **CAF subsets:** myCAF (ACTA2⁺, juxtatumoral, TGF-β/contractile), iCAF (IL-6/CXCL12/LIF, distal), apCAF (MHC-II⁺), plus dCAF, proliferating CAF, and complement-secreting csCAF — a multiplex marker panel is required to capture the full population.
- PEGPH20 lowered intratumoral interstitial fluid pressure within 2 hours, approaching the normal-pancreas range by 24 hours (mouse).

## Limitations

Review; percentages aggregate heterogeneous cohorts and models; mouse interstitial-pressure kinetics may not translate.

## Conclusion

Desmoplasia is a coupled physical, biochemical, and immune barrier: it occupies most of the tumor volume, physically excludes T cells from the tumor center, and generates the interstitial hypertension that blocks delivery. Single-target stromal depletion is insufficient and can be counterproductive — the biology behind the phase-3 stroma-modifier failures.
