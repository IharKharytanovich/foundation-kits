# Atilla 2022 — Antigen-Negative Relapse After CAR-T

Quantifies antigen escape in the setting where the target is single and defined — the clearest demonstration that losing the target antigen defeats a persistent, functional effector.

## Scope

Review of resistance mechanisms against anti-CD19 and anti-BCMA CAR-T cells, aggregating relapse and antigen-loss frequencies across cohorts.

## Quantitative Results

- **CD19-negative relapse: 9–25% of B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia** treated with CD19 CAR-T; about **33% of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma** show CD19-negative recurrence in one cohort.
- **BCMA antigen loss:** biopsy-proven at relapse in ~8% of patients; a separate cohort showed 67% with reduced BCMA intensity post-infusion; KarMMa reported antigen loss at relapse in 1/16 (6%) by IHC.
- **Overall CAR-T context:** ~81% response in ALL, ~68% in lymphoma, but long-term disease-free survival only 30–40% — antigen escape is a leading relapse driver despite CAR-T persistence.
- Corroboration: relapse after CD19/CD22 CAR-T in up to 50% of pre-B ALL by 12 months.

## Limitations

Review aggregating heterogeneous cohorts and detection methods; frequencies vary by disease and assay.

## Conclusion

Antigen escape is a leading cause of relapse after CAR-T even when the cells persist and function — direct evidence that a single-antigen effector invites escape. It is the rationale for multi-antigen targeting and for bystander-killing strategies (BiTE-armed viruses) that reach antigen-negative cells, and a key contributor to the resistance-evolution loop.
