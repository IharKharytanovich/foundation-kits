# Wang 2024 — Adenosine-Axis Blockade in the Clinic

A review aggregating the clinical trials of adenosine-pathway blockade, showing it is inactive alone but adds measurable benefit in combination and in lower-tumor-burden settings.

## Scope

Review of adenosine-pathway (CD73, A2A receptor) plus immune-checkpoint dual inhibition, aggregating trial readouts.

## Quantitative Results

- **Oleclumab (anti-CD73):**
  - HUDSON (NSCLC post-PD-(L)1 failure): ORR 4.2%, median PFS 2.63 months, median OS 12.08 months (monotherapy-level — weak).
  - **COAST** (unresectable stage III NSCLC post-chemoradiation): durvalumab + oleclumab **ORR 30.0% vs durvalumab-alone 17.9%**; 12-month PFS **62.6% vs 33.9%** — the strongest adenosine-combination signal.
  - NeoCOAST (neoadjuvant): major pathologic response 19% vs 11%; pCR 9.5% vs 3.7%.
- **Ciforadenant/CPI-444 (A2A receptor):** MORPHEUS NSCLC (atezolizumab + CPI-444) ORR 6.7%, median PFS 2.3 months — minimal as a combination add-on.
- High CD73 expression correlates with poor prognosis, nodal metastasis, and invasion in NSCLC.

## Limitations

Aggregated from multiple trials of differing design; single-agent adenosine blockade is inactive; the strongest signal (COAST) is in an earlier-stage, lower-burden, post-chemoradiation setting.

## Conclusion

Blocking the adenosine arm of metabolic hostility is inactive as monotherapy but produces a real benefit in combination with checkpoint blockade and in lower-tumor-burden settings (COAST: ORR 30% vs 17.9%, 12-month PFS 62.6% vs 33.9%). It is the most clinically validated of the metabolic-hostility countermeasures — a contrast with the clean IDO-inhibitor failure.
