# Shang 2015 — Treg Prognostic Meta-Analysis

The largest pooled analysis of tumor-infiltrating regulatory T cells, establishing both their general prognostic harm and the important tissue-dependent exceptions.

## Design & Cohort

Systematic review and meta-analysis: **76 studies, 17 cancer types, 15,512 patients**, pooling odds ratios for 5-year overall survival by FoxP3⁺ Treg density.

## Quantitative Results

- Overall: high FoxP3⁺ Treg density → lower 5-year OS, **pooled OR 1.46 (95% CI 1.19–1.78, P=0.0002)**.
- **Worse survival (Treg harmful):** cervical OR 5.11 (2.87–9.11); renal 4.26 (2.18–8.34); melanoma 2.15 (1.34–3.44); hepatocellular 1.82 (1.16–2.86); gastric 1.65 (1.08–2.52); breast 1.65 (1.13–2.41).
- **Better survival (Treg protective — suppress tumor-promoting inflammation):** colorectal OR 0.71 (0.62–0.82); head-and-neck 0.69 (0.50–0.95); esophageal 0.51 (0.33–0.79).

## Limitations

Reports odds ratios (not hazard ratios); IHC and cutoff heterogeneity across studies; the colorectal/head-and-neck reversal shows Treg prognostic value is not universal.

## Conclusion

Regulatory T cells are prognostically harmful in most solid tumors, but the effect is tumor-context-specific — protective in colorectal, head-and-neck, and esophageal cancers, where they suppress tumor-promoting inflammation. This tissue-dependence is a genuine biological nuance the knowledge base must preserve rather than encoding "Tregs are always bad." A high CD8/Treg ratio predicts the inflamed, responsive phenotype.
