# Dangaj 2019 — The CCL5 + CXCL9 Chemokine Relay

Establishes that CD8 T-cell infiltration of solid tumors requires a cooperative chemokine relay, and that its epigenetic loss is a root cause of the cold-tumor phenotype.

## Design & Methods

Pan-cancer transcriptomic association analysis (human TCGA and ovarian/melanoma cohorts) combined with mouse tumor models and in-vitro mechanistic work on chemokine regulation and T-cell chemotaxis.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- CD8 T-cell infiltration is associated with **co-expression of CCL5 and CXCL9**: CCL5 is constitutively produced by tumor cells, CXCL9 is interferon-γ-induced in myeloid/antigen-presenting cells.
- **CCL5^hi CXCL9^hi** tumors are the immunoreactive subset with prolonged survival and response to checkpoint blockade.
- Loss of tumor **CCL5 occurs via epigenetic silencing through DNA methylation**; reduced CCL5 desertifies the tumor-infiltrating-lymphocyte compartment.
- Forced re-expression of CCL5 restored Cxcl9 and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and attenuated tumor growth in an interferon-γ-dependent manner in mice.

## Limitations

Human data are association-level; mechanistic causality shown in mouse only; exact fold-changes and hazard ratios are in the full-text figures.

## Conclusion

A two-part chemokine relay — constitutive CCL5 seeds infiltration, interferon-induced CXCL9 amplifies it — is required for CD8 engraftment and immune attack. Its epigenetic silencing is a fundamental mechanism of chemokine-axis mismatch and the cold-tumor state, and the rationale for both DNMT-inhibitor re-warming and oncolytic chemokine arming.
