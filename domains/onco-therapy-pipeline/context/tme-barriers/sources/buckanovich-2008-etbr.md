# Buckanovich 2008 — The Endothelin-B Receptor Gate

Establishes a second, druggable vascular gatekeeper (independent of FasL) that blocks T-cell homing by suppressing endothelial adhesion.

## Design & Methods

Human ovarian tumor tissue analysis plus mouse tumor models, testing endothelin-B receptor (ETBR) expression, its effect on endothelial ICAM-1, and the consequences of ETBR neutralization with BQ-788.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- **ETBR is overexpressed on tumor endothelium** in human ovarian cancer; its expression inversely relates to tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes.
- ETBR signaling **suppresses endothelial ICAM-1 via nitric oxide**, preventing T-cell adhesion and homing.
- In mice, **ETBR neutralization by BQ-788 increased T-cell homing to tumors**; the effect **required ICAM-1** and enabled response to an otherwise-ineffective immunotherapy.

## Limitations

Primarily ovarian cancer; BQ-788 is a tool compound; the mechanism operates in parallel to VEGF-driven anergy and endothelial FasL.

## Conclusion

The endothelin-B receptor is a druggable endothelial gatekeeper that blocks T-cell homing by downregulating ICAM-1 through nitric oxide — a mechanistic pillar of the tumor-endothelial barrier that, like the FasL death barrier, can be reversed to restore T-cell infiltration and immunotherapy sensitivity.
