# Molon 2011 — CCL2 Nitration Traps T Cells in the Stroma

The definitive mechanistic source for chemokine nitration: a drug-reversible chemical modification that converts a T-cell chemoattractant into a myeloid one.

## Design & Methods

Mechanistic mouse plus human-tissue study. LC-MS/MS mapping of reactive-nitrogen-species (RNS) modifications on chemokines; transwell chemotaxis and calcium-flux/receptor-binding assays; in-vivo treatment with the RNS-synthesis inhibitor AT38. Models: EG7-OVA, C26GM tumors, TRAMP prostate mice; OT-I CTL adoptive transfer; human prostate/colon/liver cancer tissue.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- Intratumoral **peroxynitrite post-translationally modifies CCL2** — tyrosine nitration (mouse) and tryptophan nitration (human); CXCL12 is also nitrated.
- **Nitrated CCL2 (N-CCL2) loses the ability to attract CD8 T cells** (no transwell migration, no calcium flux in T cells) — trapping tumor-specific cytotoxic T lymphocytes in the peritumoral stroma.
- **Myeloid cells still respond** to N-CCL2 because they express far higher CCR2: CCR2 mean fluorescence intensity 13.2 ± 1.7 on CD14⁺ myeloid versus 2.3 ± 0.3 on CD8⁺ T cells (P ≤ 0.05). Nitration thus converts CCL2 from a T-cell to a myeloid/MDSC chemoattractant.
- N-CCL2 was detected in human prostate and colon cancer tissue.
- **AT38** reduced intratumoral nitrotyrosine, NOS2, and arginase (P < 0.001, n=20) and **restored CTL invasion of the tumor core**, enhancing adoptive immunotherapy.

## Limitations

Chemokines were too low-abundance to quantify directly in tumor lysate (required a nanobody detector); mostly mouse models; AT38 is not a clinical agent.

## Conclusion

Chemical nitration of CCL2 is a distinct, drug-reversible mechanism of T-cell stromal trapping — a chemokine-axis-mismatch mechanism that operates even when the chemokine is present, by disabling its T-cell-attracting signal while preserving its myeloid-recruiting one.
