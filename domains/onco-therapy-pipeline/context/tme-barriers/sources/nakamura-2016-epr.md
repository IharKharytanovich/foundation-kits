# Nakamura/Kobayashi 2016 — Is EPR Sufficient?

The review that quantifies why passive nanoparticle targeting via the enhanced-permeability-and-retention (EPR) effect is insufficient in humans.

## Scope

Review of nano-drug delivery physics, EPR magnitude, and the tumor-transport barriers that limit it.

## Quantitative Results

- EPR yields **less than 2-fold** nano-drug tumor accumulation versus critical normal organs — "generally insufficient for achieving therapeutic levels."
- **Tumor IFP 10–40 mmHg versus ~0 mmHg normal** (consistent with the seminal human measurements).
- Tumor vessel pore size **0.1–3 µm**; basement membrane **100–150 nm**; pericyte coverage **10–70%** — highly heterogeneous.
- **Tumor antibody concentration is 100–1000× lower than plasma.**
- Active enhancement approaches (near-infrared photoimmunotherapy, "SUPR") can raise delivery up to 24-fold, whereas most passive enhancement approaches only ~2-fold.

## Limitations

Does not directly quantify the human-versus-mouse EPR differential; the "unreliable in humans" conclusion rests on synthesis (IFP + poor perfusion + the ~0.7% median injected-dose figure from meta-analysis).

## Conclusion

The EPR effect, calibrated in fast-growing murine xenografts, delivers less than 2-fold accumulation and cannot achieve therapeutic tumor levels in humans, where elevated interstitial fluid pressure, dense stroma, and poor perfusion negate the leaky-vessel premise. This is the quantitative basis for treating EPR as unreal in humans and for the domain's ~0.7%-of-injected-dose delivery figure.
