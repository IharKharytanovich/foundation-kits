# Ohta 2020 — Hypoxia and Immune Exclusion

The deepest quantitative review linking tumor hypoxia to the immune-exclusion phenotype, mechanism by mechanism.

## Scope

Comprehensive review of hypoxia biology and its immunosuppressive consequences in the tumor microenvironment.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- **Physioxia (normal-tissue pO₂) is 8–100 mmHg**; venous blood 30–40, arterial 75–100 mmHg. Tumor hypoxia falls well below this (median tumor O₂ below 2%, roughly ≤10 mmHg).
- **HIF-1α is rapidly degraded in normoxia** (prolyl-hydroxylase/VHL); on re-exposure to 20% O₂, HIF-1α/1β mRNA return to normoxic levels in **under 5 minutes** — a handling caveat for tissue assays.
- Hypoxia upregulates **CD39 and CD73**, driving extracellular **adenosine** that suppresses cytotoxic T cells and NK cells via the A2A receptor.
- Hypoxia downregulates the **Kv1.3** potassium channel in T lymphocytes, impairing activation; high lactate and extracellular acidification further reduce T/NK function and survival.
- Hypoxia upregulates **PD-L1** (via HIF-1α) on tumor and myeloid cells and recruits immunosuppressive cells; it correlates with metastasis, recurrence, and shorter survival.

## Limitations

Review; the HIF-1α reoxygenation kinetics are a tissue-handling caveat rather than an in-vivo therapy target; some numbers aggregate across tumor types.

## Conclusion

Hypoxia is a common upstream determinant of immune exclusion, coupling metabolic stress to T-cell inhibition, adenosine immunosuppression, checkpoint (PD-L1) upregulation, and radioresistance — the reason vascular normalization (which improves oxygenation) is an immune-enabling, not merely delivery-enabling, intervention.
