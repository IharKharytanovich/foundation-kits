# Chang 2015 — Glucose Competition Restricts T Cells

The foundational demonstration that tumor and T cell directly compete for glucose, and the tumor's consumption starves the T cell of the fuel it needs to function.

## Design & Methods

Mechanistic study in mouse sarcoma models examining how tumor glucose consumption affects co-located T-cell metabolism and effector function.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- Tumor glucose consumption **metabolically restricts T cells**, resulting in dampened mTOR activity, reduced glycolytic capacity, and lower interferon-γ production.
- Establishes glucose as a limiting resource for which tumor and T cell directly compete; restoring T-cell glucose access or metabolic fitness improves function.

## Limitations

Mouse sarcoma models; establishes the principle qualitatively rather than tabulating intratumoral glucose concentrations.

## Conclusion

Metabolic competition for glucose is a driver of cancer progression and a direct cause of T-cell dysfunction in the tumor microenvironment — the carbon-source arm of metabolic hostility, and the basis for strategies that improve T-cell metabolic fitness. Foundational (highly cited) for the field of immunometabolism.
