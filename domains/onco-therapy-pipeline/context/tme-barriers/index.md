# TME Barriers — Why Immune Cells Fail Even After They Arrive

The tumor microenvironment blockers that sit *downstream* of delivery: even when a virus, a nanoparticle, or a T cell reaches the tumor, a stacked set of physical, vascular, metabolic, and cell-intrinsic barriers neutralizes it. This cluster holds the two-gate failure of solid-tumor immunotherapy — first the effector T cells cannot get *in* (chemokine-axis mismatch, an anergic/killing endothelium, a dense desmoplastic stroma, high interstitial fluid pressure), then, if they arrive, they cannot *function* (immunosuppressive Treg/MDSC/TAM, a metabolically hostile acidic-hypoxic-nutrient-starved milieu, epigenetically locked exhaustion) — plus antigen escape that defeats the response over time. The same physical barriers (stroma, interstitial pressure) are the biological basis of the pipeline's binding [solid-tumor delivery wall](../delivery/delivery-wall.md), so this cluster explains *why* that wall exists. It also holds the bypass attempts — vascular normalization + high-endothelial-venule induction (the most promising), and stroma modification (mostly failed in phase 3) — and marks each claim computable-vs-empirical with graded provenance. Raw per-paper breakdowns in `sources/`.
