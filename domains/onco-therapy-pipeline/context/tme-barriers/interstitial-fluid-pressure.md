# High Interstitial Fluid Pressure

[[interstitial-fluid-pressure]] is the pressure arm of the delivery wall: solid tumors are interstitially hypertensive, and the elevated pressure pushes fluid — and everything in it — outward, opposing the inward transport of any therapeutic. It is a primary physical reason the passive-targeting premise fails in humans.

## The Numbers

Direct human measurements (wick-in-needle) found tumor IFP of **4–50 mmHg, mean 20 ± 13 mmHg**, versus **~0–2 mmHg in normal tissue** — metastatic melanoma 33, colorectal liver metastases 21, primary breast 15 mmHg. Because tumor IFP approaches microvascular pressure, the transvascular pressure gradient that normally drives convection into tissue collapses, and at the tumor periphery the net flow is outward.

## Why the EPR Effect Is Unreliable in Humans

The enhanced-permeability-and-retention (EPR) effect — leaky tumor vessels letting nanoparticles accumulate — was calibrated in fast-growing murine xenografts and does not generalize. In practice EPR yields less than 2-fold tumor accumulation over critical normal organs, and the median fraction of an injected nanoparticle dose that reaches a human tumor is about **0.7%** (the figure already central to the pipeline's delivery wall). Elevated IFP, dense stroma, and poor or absent perfusion negate the leaky-vessel premise; intratumoral antibody concentration is 100–1000× lower than plasma. This is why the domain treats EPR as unreal in humans.

## The Link to Stroma and the Bypass

Interstitial hypertension is driven substantially by the [[desmoplastic-stroma-barrier]] (collagen and hyaluronan solid stress) and by abnormal, leaky vasculature. It is therefore a physical foundation of the [[solid-tumor-delivery]] wall, and the reason the 2024–26 wins are [[delivery-bypass]] strategies (aim at the liver, inject locally, hit blood cells) rather than attempts to push harder against the pressure. Vascular normalization transiently lowers IFP and improves delivery.
