# Resistance and Immune Escape Mechanisms

Targeted immunotherapy exerts strong selection pressure on tumor subpopulations, driving the rapid expansion of pre-existing resistant clones through multiple escape mechanisms.

**Antigen-negative selection (CAR-T):** Orlando et al. 2018 used single-cell sequencing to show that CD19-negative subclones exist at 0.003-1.8% VAF before CAR-T therapy and are selected by treatment. Across multiple studies (Majzner & Mackall 2018 review), 30-60% of B-ALL relapses after CD19 CAR-T are antigen-negative. Sotillo et al. 2015 identified alternative CD19 splicing (exon 2 skipping) as a mechanism producing a truncated protein invisible to the CAR construct. Shah et al. 2019 confirmed similar escape in CD22-targeted CAR-T (70% relapse rate, with antigen downregulation in most).

**MHC/HLA downregulation:** Zaretsky et al. 2016 demonstrated that acquired resistance to anti-PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors involves JAK1/JAK2 loss-of-function mutations that abolish IFN-gamma signaling, preventing MHC class I upregulation. HLA loss of heterozygosity (LOH) occurs in 40% of NSCLC (McGranahan et al. 2017 Cell 171:1259), permanently removing half the neoantigen presentation capacity. These mechanisms are distinct from antigen loss — they attack the presentation machinery rather than the target.

**Dual-antigen and combination approaches:** Sequential targeting (CD19 → CD22) fails because resistant subclones pre-exist for both targets. Simultaneous dual-antigen CAR-T (e.g., CD19/CD22 bispecific) reduces but does not eliminate escape. The biological floor is that tumors are genetically heterogeneous populations under selection, and any single-target therapy creates an evolutionary bottleneck that selects for escape variants.

**Kinetics:** Resistance clones expand rapidly: median time to antigen-negative relapse after CAR-T is 3-12 months, but the resistant subclones are detectable at diagnosis. The treatment does not create resistance — it selects for it. This means the relevant window is not when resistance appears, but when the pre-existing resistant fraction crosses a clinically relevant threshold.
