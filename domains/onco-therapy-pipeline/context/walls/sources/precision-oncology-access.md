# Precision Oncology Access Gaps

The gap between theoretical eligibility for precision/personalized therapy and actual receipt is a systemic wall that gates the pipeline's real-world throughput.

**Utilization rates:** Kris et al. 2014 (Lung Cancer Mutation Consortium, 1007 advanced NSCLC patients) found that only 36% of eligible patients received a matched targeted therapy — meaning ~64% of patients with actionable mutations did not access precision treatment. Marquart et al. 2018 estimated that only 8.3% of US cancer patients overall are eligible for an FDA-approved genome-targeted therapy, and actual utilization is lower still due to testing gaps, access delays, and insurance barriers.

**Molecular testing gaps:** Even with broad genomic profiling availability, 30-50% of advanced cancer patients in community oncology settings do not receive comprehensive genomic profiling (ASCO 2023 survey data). Turnaround time for commercial panels (FoundationOne, Tempus) is 2-3 weeks, during which disease may progress. For personalized N-of-1 therapies (neoantigen vaccines, patient-specific CAR-T), the testing-to-treatment timeline extends to 6-12 weeks.

**Regulatory pathway gaps for N-of-1 therapies:** There is no standardized FDA pathway for truly individualized (N-of-1) therapies where each patient receives a unique product. The existing IND framework requires per-product submissions. FDA CBER's 2024 draft guidance on individualized antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) represents the first regulatory framework specifically addressing N-of-1 therapies, but it covers only ASOs and requires single-patient INDs with abbreviated safety data. No analogous guidance exists for individualized mRNA vaccines, patient-specific CAR-T, or personalized gene editing constructs outside clinical trials.

**Cost barriers:** The cost of personalized cell therapies ranges from $373,000 (Kymriah) to >$500,000 (Abecma) per patient, with total cost of care often exceeding $1M. Personalized neoantigen vaccines are estimated at $100,000-$300,000 per patient in clinical trial settings. These costs are not sustainable for broad population access and are not fully covered by most insurance plans.

**NCI-MATCH evidence:** Flaherty et al. 2023 (NCI-MATCH final analysis, 6000+ patients) showed that while molecular matching is feasible at scale, objective response rates to matched targeted therapies averaged only 10-15% across arms, suggesting that molecular matching alone is insufficient — the therapeutic wall extends beyond testing access to the biology of response.
