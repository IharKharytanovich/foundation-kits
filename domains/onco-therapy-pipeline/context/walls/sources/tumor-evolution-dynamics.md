# Tumor Evolution Dynamics and Treatment-Cycle Timing

The central tension of personalized cancer therapy is that the tumor's mutational and antigenic landscape can shift within the time required to manufacture a personalized treatment.

**TRACERx longitudinal evidence:** Jamal-Hanjani et al. 2017 (TRACERx 100, NSCLC) demonstrated that 30% of tumor mutations are subclonal at diagnosis and that branched evolution continues during the treatment interval. Abbosh et al. 2023 (TRACERx 421) extended this to show that phylogenetic diversity increases measurably between biopsy and surgery (median 12 weeks), with new subclones detectable in 15-25% of patients. The median doubling time for a nascent subclone is 2-4 weeks in aggressive tumors.

**Antigen-negative escape under therapy:** Majzner & Mackall 2018 reviewed antigen loss as a mechanism of CAR-T resistance: 30-60% of B-ALL relapses after CD19 CAR-T are CD19-negative, and these antigen-negative subclones are detectable at 0.003-1.8% VAF before therapy (Orlando et al. 2018 Nat Med 24:1504). The therapy acts as a selection pressure that amplifies pre-existing resistant clones.

**Implications for personalized therapy cycling:** A personalized mRNA neoantigen vaccine cycle is currently ~4 weeks from diff to dose (BioNTech/Moderna timelines). Within this window, the tumor can undergo measurable clonal evolution (TRACERx data), potentially rendering the vaccine's target neoantigens subclonal or absent. The faster the treatment cycle, the more likely the targets remain relevant — but even at the aspirational <1-week timeline, pre-existing resistant subclones are already present.

**Quantitative tension:** If a subclone doubles every 2-4 weeks and the treatment cycle is 4 weeks, a subclone at 1% frequency at biopsy could reach 2-4% by dosing — still below the typical detection threshold of the next ctDNA monitoring cycle. The mismatch between evolution speed and measurement resolution compounds the timing wall.
