# ctDNA as Surrogate Endpoint and the Measurement Gap

Two measurement gaps constrain the pipeline's efficacy stage: (1) ctDNA is prognostic but not an accepted surrogate for OS/DFS, and (2) in-vivo gene editing efficiency in solid tumors is not directly measurable.

**ctDNA surrogacy status:** Tie et al. 2022 (DYNAMIC trial, 455 stage II colon cancer patients) demonstrated that ctDNA-guided adjuvant therapy reduced chemotherapy use by 50% without compromising recurrence-free survival, establishing ctDNA as a clinically useful prognostic biomarker. However, the FDA's 2022 draft guidance explicitly states that ctDNA clearance is not yet validated as a surrogate endpoint for overall survival (OS) or disease-free survival (DFS). The guidance permits ctDNA as an exploratory endpoint and encourages its co-development with therapeutics, but regulatory approval decisions cannot yet be based on ctDNA response alone. Henriksen et al. 2024 (meta-analysis of 12,000+ patients across 20 trials) confirmed the prognostic value of ctDNA dynamics (HR 3.1-5.2 for OS) but noted that the relationship between ctDNA clearance and OS varies by tumor type and treatment modality, preventing universal surrogacy.

**In-vivo editing measurement in solid tumors:** For gene editing therapies targeting solid tumors, measuring the fraction of tumor cells successfully edited in vivo remains aspirational. Current approaches rely on: (1) pre/post biopsy with NGS (invasive, sampling bias, tumor heterogeneity), (2) circulating biomarker proxies (protein levels for knockout targets — only applicable when the edited gene produces a measurable circulating product), or (3) imaging surrogates (FDG-PET for metabolic response, not editing-specific). Verve Therapeutics' HEART-1 trial (2024) demonstrated measurable PCSK9 reduction after in-vivo liver base editing (Musunuru et al. 2021 showed 60% editing in non-human primates), but the liver is uniquely accessible — editing efficiency measurement in dispersed solid tumor cells has no validated approach.

**Net impact:** The pipeline cannot close the efficacy feedback loop without measuring what it did. ctDNA tells you whether tumor burden changed, but not whether editing succeeded. Protein biomarkers work only for specific targets. The measurement gap is structural and unsettled.
