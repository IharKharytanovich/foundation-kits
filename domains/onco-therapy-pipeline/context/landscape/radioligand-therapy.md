# Radioligand Therapy

[[radioligand-therapy]] pairs a tumor-receptor-targeting ligand with a cytotoxic radioisotope, irradiating the tumor from within by short-range emission. It is a mature **approved** class delivered systemically by IV infusion.

**Maturity: approved. Personalization type: matched** (companion-imaging-selected).

## Pluvicto / VISION

**177Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto)** targets PSMA on prostate cancer cells with a lutetium-177 beta-emitter. In the **VISION trial**, adding it to standard care in PSMA-positive mCRPC **prolonged median overall survival to 15.3 vs 11.3 months** and rPFS to 8.7 vs 3.4 months (see `sources/pluvicto-vision-trial.md`). FDA-approved 2022; indication expanded to the pre-chemotherapy setting March 28, 2025. The earlier **177Lu-DOTATATE (Lutathera, 2018)** established the class in somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumors.

## The Theranostic Pair

The same targeting scaffold carries a **diagnostic** isotope (68Ga/18F-PSMA PET) to confirm target expression and select patients, then a **therapeutic** isotope (177Lu, or alpha-emitting 225Ac) to treat — a "see-it, treat-it" loop. Patient selection is imaging-driven, making the modality matched rather than bespoke.

## Delivery Contrast

Radioligands reach **disseminated, radiographically receptor-avid** disease through the bloodstream — a systemic route that does not depend on penetrating a solid tumor's interstitium. This is precisely the delivery problem that blocks [[genome-editing-modality]] in solid tumors: a small radioligand distributes to receptor-positive lesions body-wide, whereas an editor payload cannot be delivered into solid-tumor cells in vivo. Radioligand therapy therefore sidesteps, by physics of the payload, the wall that keeps in-vivo genetic editing preclinical.
