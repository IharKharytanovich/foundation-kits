# Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADC)

[[antibody-drug-conjugate]] couples a tumor-antigen-directed monoclonal antibody to a cytotoxic payload through a linker, concentrating chemotherapy on antigen-expressing cells while sparing most normal tissue. It is one of the fastest-growing classes of the approved backbone: **15 ADCs are FDA-approved as of 2025** (see `sources/adc-approvals-2025.md`).

**Maturity: approved. Personalization type: matched** (a patient is matched by companion-diagnostic antigen expression — HER2 IHC, Trop-2 — not by bespoke per-patient manufacture).

## The Deruxtecan Inflection

The **deruxtecan** payload platform (a membrane-permeable topoisomerase-I inhibitor) produces a **bystander effect**: payload released in a targeted cell diffuses into neighboring antigen-negative cells, defeating antigen heterogeneity. This is why **trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu)** works not only in HER2-positive but in **HER2-low and HER2-ultralow** breast cancer, and why **datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway, Trop-2)** was approved January 17, 2025 for HR+/HER2- disease.

## Therapeutic-Index Physics

The class is defined by linker chemistry: a stable linker in circulation plus efficient intracellular payload release. Premature cleavage drives off-tumor toxicity — interstitial lung disease is the signature deruxtecan-class risk. ADC design (drug-to-antibody ratio, linker stability, payload potency) is the tunable that sets the therapeutic window.

## Relation to Other Targeted Modalities

Like the off-the-shelf [[bispecific-t-cell-engager]], an ADC is a matched, non-bespoke biologic that exploits a tumor-restricted surface antigen — but it delivers a chemical payload directly rather than recruiting the immune system. Both are limited by the same requirement: a differentially expressed, internalizable (ADC) or redirectable (BiTE) surface target.
