# Bispecific T-Cell Engagers (BiTE)

[[bispecific-t-cell-engager]] is an off-the-shelf biologic that bridges a tumor antigen and CD3 on T cells, forcing an immune synapse that redirects a patient's polyclonal T-cell repertoire onto the tumor. No patient-specific manufacturing is required — it is dosed as an IV infusion.

**Maturity: approved. Personalization type: off-the-shelf.**

## 2024 — Crossing into Solid Tumors

The class was previously confined to hematologic malignancies (blinatumomab, CD19xCD3, 2014). In May 2024 **tarlatamab (Imdelltra)** — a DLL3xCD3 engager — became the **first BiTE approved for a solid tumor** (extensive-stage small cell lung cancer): DeLLphi-301 reported **ORR 40%** and **median overall survival 14.3 months** in a disease whose historical median OS is ~5 months (see `sources/fda-tarlatamab-2024.md`).

BiTE builds on the same T-cell-redirection principle that [[adoptive-cell-therapy]] achieves with an engineered cell product, but delivers it as an off-the-shelf reagent instead of a bespoke manufactured cell — trading persistence for immediacy and scalability.

extends:: [[adoptive-cell-therapy]]

## Position Among 2024 Solid-Tumor T-Cell Approvals

Tarlatamab (BiTE) arrived the same year as **afami-cel/Tecelra** (TCR-T, `sources/fda-afamicel-2024.md`) and lifileucel/Amtagvi (TIL) — three different ways to point T cells at a solid tumor. BiTE is the only one of the three that is off-the-shelf; the cell therapies are bespoke and multi-week to manufacture, whereas a BiTE is on-the-shelf and immediately dosable.

## Constraints

- **Cytokine release syndrome** (~51% of tarlatamab patients) and neurotoxicity require step-up dosing and inpatient monitoring, capping outpatient throughput.
- **Target dependence**: efficacy requires a redirectable, tumor-restricted surface antigen (DLL3 for SCLC); antigen-negative escape drives relapse.
- **Transient engagement**: unlike a persisting CAR-T cell, a BiTE clears with the infusion and needs continuous or repeated dosing.
