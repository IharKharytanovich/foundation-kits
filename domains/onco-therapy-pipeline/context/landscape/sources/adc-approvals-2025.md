# ADC Approval Landscape, 2024-2025

Antibody-drug conjugates couple a tumor-antigen-directed monoclonal antibody to a cytotoxic payload via a cleavable or non-cleavable linker, delivering chemotherapy selectively to antigen-expressing cells.

## Approval Count and Recent Additions

- **15 ADCs FDA-approved** as of 2025 (Jang et al. 2025); ~21 approved globally across regulators.
- **Datopotamab deruxtecan-dlnk (Datroway, Daiichi Sankyo)** — Trop-2-directed — approved **January 17, 2025** for HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer.
- **Telisotuzumab vedotin** (c-Met-directed) approved 2025 for NSCLC.
- **Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu)** label expanded to HER2-low and HER2-ultralow breast cancer, and granted a tumor-agnostic HER2-positive indication.

## Mechanistic Themes

- **Bystander effect**: membrane-permeable payloads (e.g., deruxtecan, a topoisomerase-I inhibitor) diffuse from the targeted cell into neighboring antigen-negative cells, addressing antigen heterogeneity — the reason deruxtecan-class ADCs work in HER2-low disease.
- **Target-directed, not patient-bespoke**: ADCs are off-the-shelf biologics matched to a patient by companion-diagnostic antigen expression (HER2 IHC, Trop-2), not manufactured per patient.
- **Therapeutic index** is set by linker stability and payload potency; premature linker cleavage drives off-tumor toxicity (interstitial lung disease is the class-defining deruxtecan risk).

## Significance

ADCs are among the fastest-growing approved backbone classes: the deruxtecan platform converted "undruggable low-antigen" tumors into treatable ones and pushed ADCs from niche to front-line across breast, lung, and bladder cancers.
