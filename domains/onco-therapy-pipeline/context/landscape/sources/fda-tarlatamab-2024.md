# FDA Approval — Tarlatamab (Imdelltra), 2024

On May 16, 2024 the FDA granted **accelerated approval** to tarlatamab-dlle (Imdelltra, Amgen) for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy.

## Modality and Mechanism

Tarlatamab is a **bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE)** that binds **DLL3** (delta-like ligand 3, expressed on ~85–96% of SCLC cells) on the tumor with one arm and **CD3** on T cells with the other, forcing an immune synapse that redirects polyclonal T-cell cytotoxicity onto the tumor. It is an **off-the-shelf** biologic — no patient-specific manufacturing — dosed by IV infusion.

## Key Trial Data (DeLLphi-301)

- **Objective response rate: 40%** (95% CI 29–52) at the 10 mg dose.
- **Median overall survival: 14.3 months.**
- **Median duration of response: ~9.7 months** (durable responses in a historically ~5-month-median-OS disease).
- Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) in ~51% of patients (mostly grade 1–2); neurologic events including ICANS required step-up dosing and monitoring.

## Significance

Tarlatamab is the **first BiTE (and first T-cell engager of any kind) approved for a solid tumor**, extending the T-cell-redirection principle — previously confined to hematologic malignancies (blinatumomab, CD19×CD3) — into thoracic oncology. It validates DLL3 as a redirectable solid-tumor target and establishes CRS-managed BiTE dosing as clinically deliverable outside heme.
