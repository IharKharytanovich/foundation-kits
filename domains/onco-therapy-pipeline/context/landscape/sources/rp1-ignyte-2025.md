# Oncolytic Virotherapy — RP1 / IGNYTE and the T-VEC Baseline

An oncolytic virus is engineered to selectively replicate in and lyse tumor cells while sparing normal tissue; the resulting immunogenic cell death releases tumor antigens and danger signals, converting an immunologically "cold" tumor into a "hot" one — an **in-situ vaccination** effect that pairs with checkpoint blockade.

## RP1 / IGNYTE

- **RP1 (vusolimogene oderparepvec)** — an HSV-1-based oncolytic expressing GM-CSF and a fusogenic glycoprotein (GALV-GP-R-), delivered intratumorally, combined with nivolumab (anti-PD-1).
- **IGNYTE trial** (Wong et al., JCO 2025): in anti-PD-1-failed advanced melanoma, confirmed **ORR 32.9%** (95% CI 25.2-41.3), with ~15% complete responses; responses occurred in both injected and non-injected (abscopal) lesions.
- **Regulatory status**: the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (twice) for RP1 + nivolumab in advanced melanoma — **not approved** as of 2025; the confirmatory trial is ongoing.

## T-VEC Baseline

**Talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC)**, approved in 2015, remains the **only FDA-approved oncolytic virus** — an HSV-1 vector expressing GM-CSF for unresectable melanoma. It established intratumoral oncolytic delivery and the abscopal/in-situ-vaccination rationale that RP1 and next-generation vectors build on.

## Modality Character

- Maturity: **approved (T-VEC) to clinical (RP1 and pipeline)**.
- Personalization type: **off-the-shelf** vector, delivered **locally/intratumorally** — it bypasses the systemic solid-tumor delivery wall by direct injection rather than IV biodistribution.
- The therapeutic effect depends on releasing the patient's own tumor antigens in situ, mechanistically adjacent to individualized neoantigen vaccination.
