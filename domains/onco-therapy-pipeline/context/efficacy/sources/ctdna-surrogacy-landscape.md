# ctDNA Surrogacy Landscape

## Regulatory Status (as of 2025)

The FDA released a draft guidance in 2022 titled "Use of Circulating Tumor DNA for Early-Stage Solid Tumors: Clinical Trial Design, Reference Standard Considerations" acknowledging ctDNA as a prognostic biomarker but explicitly not validating it as a surrogate endpoint for OS or DFS. No regulatory agency has granted ctDNA formal surrogate status for survival endpoints in any solid tumor indication as of 2025.

The Prentice criteria for surrogate validation require that the treatment effect on the surrogate fully captures the treatment effect on the clinical endpoint. For ctDNA, this condition remains unmet: ctDNA clearance correlates with improved outcomes but the quantitative mapping (how much ctDNA reduction translates to how much survival gain) varies by tumor type, assay, and treatment modality.

## Key Evidence

**DYNAMIC trial** (Tie et al, NEJM 2022): ctDNA-guided adjuvant chemotherapy in stage II colon cancer. ctDNA-guided management reduced adjuvant chemotherapy use from 28% to 15% without compromising 2-year recurrence-free survival (93.5% vs 92.4%, non-inferior). Landmark study showing ctDNA can safely de-escalate treatment, but the design tests ctDNA as a decision tool, not as a validated surrogate endpoint.

**IMvigor010** (Powles et al, Nature Medicine 2021): ctDNA-stratified analysis in muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma. ctDNA-positive patients showed DFS benefit from adjuvant atezolizumab (HR 0.58); ctDNA-negative patients showed no benefit (HR 1.07). Demonstrates ctDNA as a patient-selection biomarker, not a surrogate for OS.

**Meta-analytic evidence** (Henriksen et al, JAMA Oncology 2024): Pooled analysis across solid tumors shows ctDNA positivity post-surgery is strongly prognostic (HR for recurrence 5.0–11.0 across tumor types), but prognostic value is not surrogacy — a biomarker can predict outcomes without capturing the treatment effect.

## The Validation Gap

The gap is structural, not evidential:
1. **Assay heterogeneity** — tumor-informed (Signatera, RaDaR) vs tumor-agnostic (Guardant Reveal) panels detect different ctDNA fractions at different sensitivities (tumor-informed: LOD ~0.01% VAF; tumor-agnostic: LOD ~0.1% VAF).
2. **Shedding biology** — some tumors (microsatellite-stable CRC, low-grade glioma, renal cell carcinoma) shed ctDNA poorly; sensitivity drops below 50% in early-stage low-shedding tumors.
3. **No trial-level surrogacy** — meta-analytic surrogacy (Buyse framework) requires demonstrating that the treatment effect on ctDNA clearance predicts the treatment effect on OS across multiple randomized trials. Only colorectal cancer has enough trial data to attempt this, and the R² at the trial level remains below the 0.7 threshold for reliable surrogacy.
4. **Clearance dynamics** — the optimal timepoint for ctDNA assessment (post-surgery, post-cycle 1, post-treatment) and the definition of "clearance" (undetectable vs log-fold reduction) are not standardized.

## Quantitative Benchmarks

- ctDNA detection sensitivity, tumor-informed assays: 90–95% in stage III CRC, 50–70% in stage II CRC (Reinert 2024)
- ctDNA detection sensitivity, tumor-agnostic assays: 55–75% in stage II–III CRC (Guardant ECLIPSE, 2023)
- Post-surgical ctDNA positivity as predictor of recurrence: sensitivity 48–88%, specificity 92–98% depending on tumor type and assay (Henriksen 2024 pooled)
- Median lead time of ctDNA detection over radiographic recurrence: 4–11 months (tumor-type dependent)
