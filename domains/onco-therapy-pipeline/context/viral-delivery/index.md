# Viral & Vector Delivery — Getting the Virus, Gene, or Cell to the Tumor

How replicating oncolytic viruses and engineered gene/cell vectors physically reach cancer, what blocks them, and how the 2023–2026 field routes around those blocks. The binding constraint here is **systemic delivery of a replication-competent virus**: neutralizing antibodies, complement, and hepatosplenic sequestration strip an intravenous dose before it reaches a solid tumor, so almost every clinical win is either **injected locally** (intratumoral/intravesical/intraperitoneal) or **rides a carrier** (blood cells, retargeted vector, in-vivo-programmed T cells) rather than surviving the bloodstream. This cluster covers the neutralizing-antibody wall and route-dependence, the bypass strategies (cell carriers, local payload secretion), transgene "arming" (cytokine / BiTE / checkpoint / chemokine-FLT3L), the oncolytic-virus platform landscape by virus family and regulatory status, and the non-viral/retargeted vector frontier (engineered VLPs for transient editor delivery, capsid/envelope-retargeted AAV and lentivirus, in-vivo CAR-T). It mirrors the domain's central pattern — wins **bypass** the delivery wall, they do not break it. Raw source breakdowns live in `sources/`.
