# Moehler 2019 (TRAVERSE) — IV Vaccinia Fails in HCC

A randomized readout showing that intravenous oncolytic vaccinia, given to a population primed against it, adds nothing — a clinical marker of the systemic-delivery wall.

## Design & Cohort

Randomized, multicenter phase 2b. **129 patients** with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma after sorafenib failure, randomized ~2:1 to pexa-vec + best supportive care (BSC, n=86) versus BSC alone (n=43). Pexa-vec (JX-594, Wyeth-strain vaccinia, thymidine-kinase-inactivated, GM-CSF-armed) given as a single **intravenous 1×10⁹ pfu** on day 1 plus up to 5 intratumoral injections.

## Quantitative Results

- **Median overall survival: 4.2 months (pexa-vec + BSC) versus 4.4 months (BSC); HR 1.19 (95% CI 0.78–1.80); p=0.428** — no benefit.
- No complete or partial responses in either arm. Disease control rate 13% versus 18%.

## Author-Stated Explanation

The refractory, immunosuppressed second-line population and **neutralizing anti-vaccinia antibodies blunting the intravenous delivery** were cited as reasons the earlier intratumoral phase-2 signal (Heo 2013) did not reproduce.

## Limitations

Heavily pretreated end-stage population; combined IV + intratumoral dosing confounds attribution; the IV component specifically is where antibody blunting bites.

## Conclusion

Intravenous oncolytic vaccinia added no survival benefit in HCC, consistent with the systemic-delivery wall: a virus most of the population is immune to (via smallpox vaccination) is neutralized before it can act. Together with the negative phase-3 PHOCUS, TRAVERSE marks the repeated failure of systemic vaccinia and the reason the field turned to local delivery, carriers, and shielding.
