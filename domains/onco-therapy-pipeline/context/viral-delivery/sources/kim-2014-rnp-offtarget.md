# Kim 2014 — RNP vs Plasmid, the Origin Datum

The paper that first quantified why delivering Cas9 as short-lived protein–RNA ribonucleoprotein produces less off-target editing than delivering it as DNA — the causal link being editor exposure time.

## Design & Methods

Compared purified Cas9 ribonucleoprotein (RNP) versus plasmid-encoded Cas9 in K562, BJ fibroblasts, and H9 embryonic stem cells at the CCR5, AAVS1, and C4BPB loci. On- and off-target editing measured by T7E1 mismatch assay and targeted deep sequencing; Cas9 protein persistence tracked over time.

## Quantitative Results

- On-target editing up to **79%** with RNP.
- **On/off-target ratio (AAVS1-S3): RNP 114 and 279 versus plasmid 12 and 22 — i.e. 9.5× and 13× more specific.**
- RNP off-target editing was **undetectable** at sites where plasmid delivery gave 6% and 1%.
- **Cas9 was barely detectable by 24 hours after RNP delivery versus several days after plasmid delivery** — the mechanistic cause.
- The authors state explicitly: "continuous expression of Cas9 and sgRNA... gives rise to the accumulation of off-target mutations."

## Limitations

In-vitro; T7E1 assay floor ~1% (deep sequencing is more sensitive); locus-dependent magnitudes; RNP delivery efficiency itself varies by cell type.

## Conclusion

Cas9 delivered as ribonucleoprotein is ~10-fold more specific than plasmid-encoded Cas9 because the protein is cleared within ~24 hours rather than expressed for days — establishing the exposure-time mechanism that the eVLP, VSV-G-vesicle, and mRNA delivery formats later exploited. It is the foundational data point for the transient-editor-advantage principle.
