# Garcia-Carbonero 2017 — EnAd, Best Human IV-Delivery Proof

The clearest clinical evidence that an oncolytic virus can be delivered to solid tumors intravenously — the positive counterpoint to the vaccinia IV failures.

## Design & Cohort

Mechanism-of-action phase 1. **12 patients** (colorectal 5, renal cell 3, NSCLC 2, urothelial 2) received **intravenous enadenotucirev (EnAd)** — a blood-stable chimeric Ad11p/Ad3 group-B oncolytic adenovirus — at 1×10¹² viral particles on days 1, 3, and 5, followed by primary tumor resection on days 8–25.

## Quantitative Results

- **EnAd genome detected in tumor of 11/12 IV patients** (5/5 colorectal, 3/3 renal, 2/2 NSCLC).
- Nuclear hexon immunohistochemistry was confined to epithelial tumor cells; **normal tissue was negative** — tumor-selective delivery.
- Blood showed an ~80-fold decrease over 8 hours → circulating half-life ≈ 20 minutes.
- Baseline anti-EnAd IgG <1:11; post-IV titers peaked around day 22, and two-thirds stayed <1:100.
- High intratumoral CD8 in 8/10 colorectal cases.

## Limitations

Small n; biopsy positivity is a presence/absence readout, not a percent-of-injected-dose; group-B adenovirus is specifically blood-stable (Ad5 and vaccinia are not), so the result does not generalize across virus families.

## Conclusion

Described by the authors as "the first clinical trial to clearly demonstrate consistent delivery of an oncolytic virus to tumors following intravenous administration." The chassis matters: the blood-stable group-B adenovirus reaches tumor in 11/12 patients where double-deleted vaccinia reaches only 2/8 — the empirical basis for saying IV delivery is real but grossly chassis-dependent.
