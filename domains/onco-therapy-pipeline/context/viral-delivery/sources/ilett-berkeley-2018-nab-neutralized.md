# Ilett/Berkeley 2018 — Antibody-Neutralized Reovirus Is Still Effective

The strongest statement that pre-existing neutralizing antibodies are not simply a barrier to oncolytic reovirus: antibody-bound virus is taken up by monocytes and still delivered, functional, to tumor.

## Design & Methods

Preclinical study using human cells and in-vivo models. Reovirus was pre-formed into complexes with neutralizing antibody (NAb), then exposed to human monocytes. Uptake mechanism was dissected with Fcγ-receptor blockade. Delivered virus was tested for the ability to infect and lyse tumor cells.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- Human monocytes **internalize pre-formed reovirus–antibody (NAb) complexes**, with uptake mediated at least in part by **Fcγ receptors, notably FcγRIII (CD16)**.
- These monocytes then **deliver functional, replicative reovirus to tumor cells**, causing tumor-cell infection and lysis.
- Antibody binding does **not** inactivate the therapy — and can even **facilitate** carrier uptake via the Fc receptor.

## Companion Findings (same group)

- Ilett 2009 (Gene Ther 16:689–699): in virus-immune mice, neat reovirus was completely ineffective while dendritic-cell and T-cell carriers remained therapeutic; mature DCs outperformed T cells.
- Ilett 2011 (Clin Cancer Res 17:2767–2776): electron microscopy showed DCs internalize reovirus (T cells surface-retain it); DC-loaded reovirus killed melanoma even in human neutralizing serum, where free virus was blocked — internalization is the protective mechanism.

## Limitations

Preclinical (human cells + in-vivo models), single virus. Does not by itself establish clinical efficacy of the delivered virus.

## Conclusion

Directly challenges the dogma that pre-existing neutralizing antibodies block oncolytic virotherapy. The virus–antibody complex is a substrate for Fc-receptor-mediated carrier uptake, so cell carriage can turn an apparent immunological liability into a delivery route — the mechanistic core of the cell-carrier bypass.
