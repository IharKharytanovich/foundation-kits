# Olarewaju 2024 — αFAP-Nanobody AAV to the Tumor Stroma

The strongest oncology-delivery demonstration of AAV retargeting: an AAV precisely redirected to the tumor microenvironment with simultaneous liver/spleen de-targeting.

## Design & Methods

Anti-fibroblast-activation-protein (FAP) nanobodies displayed on a modified AAV2 capsid. FAP is expressed on tumor-associated stroma across many cancers. Tested in vitro on FAP⁺ versus FAP-negative cells and in vivo (1×10¹¹ vg intravenously per mouse, n=3–4) in mammary-fat-pad and subcutaneous tumor models.

## Quantitative Results

- **In vitro:** 23–80× increase in selective transduction of FAP⁺ tumor cells; HT1080-huFAP selectivity 33.6× over FAP-negative control; the improved AAV-αFAP_Nb2 gave 22× on-target specificity; off-target <5%.
- **In vivo:** mammary-fat-pad tumor 6.4× more genomes than AAV2 with 10× lower liver; subcutaneous tumor 5.5× more than AAV2 with 9× lower liver; spleen 19.2× reduced. Overall **>5× tumor / 8.8× fewer liver genomes**.

## Limitations

Targets the FAP⁺ stroma, not tumor cells directly; small cohorts; AAV2 base (subject to seroprevalence and neutralization).

## Conclusion

AAV can be precisely redirected to the tumor microenvironment (via the stromal FAP antigen) while simultaneously de-targeting the liver and spleen — a viable oncology-delivery chassis and a demonstration that capsid retargeting turns the vector's default hepatic biodistribution into a tumor-directed one. It also connects to the stromal-targeting logic of FAP-BiTE-armed oncolytic viruses.
