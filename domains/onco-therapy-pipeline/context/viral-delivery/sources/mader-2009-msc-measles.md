# Mader 2009 — MSC Carriers Protect Measles from Antibody

The definitive controlled naked-versus-carrier experiment: in antibody-positive hosts, naked virus is inert while cell-carried virus doubles survival.

## Design & Cohort

Orthotopic ovarian cancer in measles-immune mice; n=11–12 per group. Treatment with 10⁵ measles-infected mesenchymal stem cells (MSC) or 10⁵ TCID₅₀ naked measles virus. Anti-measles serum titers measured against infectious-center output.

## Quantitative Results

In antibody-positive mice:

| Treatment | Median survival | p vs saline |
|---|---|---|
| Saline | 31 days | — |
| Naked MV + antibodies | 31 days | no benefit |
| MV/MSC + antibodies | 66 days | p<0.0001 |

- **MSC delivery doubled survival (66 vs 31 days);** naked virus was inert once neutralized.
- At 69 EU/mL anti-measles serum, naked virus was fully neutralized (0 syncytia) while MSC-delivered virus retained ~40% infectious centers — **~32-fold neutralizing-antibody protection**.

## Limitations

Single tumor model; preclinical; MSC-carrier manufacturing/expansion time is a practical constraint (seen clinically in CELYVIR's screen-fail rate).

## Conclusion

Carrier cells shield oncolytic virus from neutralizing antibodies (~32-fold) and restore efficacy in seropositive hosts, doubling survival where naked virus does nothing. This is the controlled quantitative basis for the carrier-cell bypass and for the clinical MSC (CELYVIR), neural-stem-cell, and CIK/T-cell delivery programs.
