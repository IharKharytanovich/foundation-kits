# Doronin 2009 — PEG Coating De-Targets the Liver

Quantifies how much surface PEGylation can shift an oncolytic adenovirus away from its default hepatic sink, and shows the de-targeting translates into better tumor efficacy.

## Design & Methods

Compared 5-kDa versus 20-kDa PEG conjugation to oncolytic adenovirus. Models: nude, C57BL/6, and ICR mice; Hep3B and LNCaP tumor xenografts; single intravenous dose of 5×10¹⁰ viral genomes. Liver transduction, hepatotoxicity (ALT), Kupffer-cell interaction, and tumor efficacy measured over time.

## Quantitative Results

- **Liver transduction reduction (20-kDa PEG):** day 1 **57-fold**, day 3 **90-fold**, day 7 2.3-fold, day 12 5.5-fold — the strongest de-targeting in the first days after dosing.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** ALT was not different from PBS (p=0.93) — de-targeting without liver injury.
- **Kupffer interaction:** pre-dosing with unmodified virus produced an 11-fold increase in hepatic luciferase (Kupffer saturation effect); PEGylated virus only 4.7-fold.
- **Efficacy:** against Hep3B tumors, PEG-20K virus was significantly better than unmodified (p=0.02); against LNCaP both cured (p=0.99).

## Limitations

Liver sinusoid fenestrae differ across species, so the optimal PEG size may differ clinically. PEGylation can also reduce infectivity if over-conjugated; the 20-kDa/day-1–3 window is where de-targeting outweighs the infectivity cost.

## Conclusion

High-molecular-weight PEGylation cuts oncolytic-adenovirus liver transduction by 1.5–2 logs in the critical first days and improves intravenous efficacy — a concrete demonstration that virion surface shielding de-targets the reticuloendothelial sink, the shielding half of the systemic-delivery bypass.
