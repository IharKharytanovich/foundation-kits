# Friedman 2021 — G207 in Pediatric High-Grade Glioma

A pediatric oncolytic-HSV readout showing both a survival signal and clear cold-to-hot immune conversion, with remarkable safety adjacent to eloquent brain.

## Design & Cohort

Phase 1, 3+3 dose-escalation, 4 cohorts. **12 patients**, ages 7–18, with recurrent or progressive supratentorial high-grade glioma (10 glioblastoma). Intratumoral **G207** (a second-generation HSV-1: both γ34.5 copies deleted + ICP6 inactivated) at 1×10⁷ or 1×10⁸ pfu, with or without a single 5 Gy radiation dose within 24 hours.

## Quantitative Results

- **Median OS 12.2 months** (95% CI 8.0–16.4) versus ~5.6 months historical — roughly doubled.
- **4 of 11 patients alive at 18 months**; radiographic stable disease in 7/12 at 1 month.
- **Cold-to-hot conversion:** matched biopsies showed marked increases in CD3⁺/CD4⁺/CD8⁺ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes at 2–9 months, including distant from the inoculation sites.
- **Safety: zero grade 3–4 G207-attributable adverse events; no dose-limiting toxicities; no viral shedding.**

## Limitations

n=12; single-arm; historical comparator; mixed doses ± radiation.

## Conclusion

G207 roughly doubled median survival in pediatric high-grade glioma and turned "immunologically quiet" tumors into T-cell-infiltrated ones, with an exceptional safety profile next to eloquent brain — evidence that oncolytic HSV's benefit in glioma is immune-mediated (the cold-to-hot conversion) and the basis for ongoing phase 2 pediatric programs. It holds FDA Breakthrough and rare-pediatric-disease designations.
