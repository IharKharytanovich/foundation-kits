# Fares 2021 — Neural-Stem-Cell-Delivered Oncolytic Adenovirus in Glioma

The clinical proof that a cell carrier can deliver an oncolytic virus into a solid-tumor site (the glioma resection cavity) with a promising survival signal.

## Design & Cohort

First-in-human, phase 1, dose-escalation. **12 patients** with newly diagnosed malignant glioma received neural stem cells (NSCs) loaded with the oncolytic adenovirus CRAd-S-pk7, injected into the walls of the resection cavity, across 3 dose cohorts (up to 1.5×10⁸ NSCs + 1.875×10¹¹ viral particles). MTD not reached; no dose-limiting toxicity.

## Quantitative Results

- **Median PFS 9.05 months; median OS 18.4 months.**
- MGMT-unmethylated subset (n=9): PFS 8.8 months, OS 18.0 months — versus historical ~12–14 months for this poor-prognosis group.
- Viral DNA undetectable in brain at 4–24 months (no persistence).
- Anti-adenovirus neutralizing-antibody titers low (1:20) by day 14 at the first dose level.
- Increased CD8⁺ T cells; neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio dropped (p=0.0027).

## Limitations

Small n; single-arm; historical comparator; local (cavity) delivery rather than systemic.

## Conclusion

Neural-stem-cell delivery of an oncolytic adenovirus into the glioma cavity was safe, elicited an immune-mediated anti-glioma response, and reached a median OS of 18.4 months (18.0 in the MGMT-unmethylated subset) — the strongest clinical carrier-cell survival signal, complementing the MSC (CELYVIR) and CIK/T-cell carrier data.
