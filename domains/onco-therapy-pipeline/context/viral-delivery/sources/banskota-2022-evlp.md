# Banskota & Liu 2022 — Fourth-Generation eVLPs

The template paper for transient, DNA-free delivery of gene editors: an engineered virus-like particle that packages editor protein as ribonucleoprotein and edits in vivo at therapeutic efficiency with minimal off-target.

## Design & Methods

Engineered virus-like particles (eVLPs) packaging adenine base editor (ABE) or Cas9 as **protein–RNA ribonucleoprotein** (no DNA). Three bottlenecks were engineered across generations v1→v4: cargo packaging, protease-cleavage/release, and cargo localization. Models: HEK293T (in vitro), mouse liver (Pcsk9), mouse retina (Rpe65, LCA/rd12 blindness model), mouse brain. Off-target assessed by CIRCLE-seq-nominated loci; compared against AAV8, lentivirus, and plasmid.

## Quantitative Results

- **v4 eVLPs were ~26-fold** more efficient at Pcsk9 liver editing than a first-generation design (5–40-fold higher editing across targets).
- **63% base editing in mouse liver** from a **single systemic injection**; serum PCSK9 protein reduced **78%**.
- Retina (Rpe65): therapeutically relevant A•T→G•C conversion with partial visual-function rescue in a genetic-blindness model.
- **Doses:** ~4×10¹¹ to 7×10¹¹ particles for systemic liver studies.
- **Off-target:** Cas-dependent off-target editing 12–900× lower than plasmid; Cas-independent (R-loop) >100× lower; in-vivo liver no off-target above background where AAV8 dual-vector gave 0.1–0.3%; RNA off-target eliminated versus an ABE-lentivirus.
- Durability: ~1 week (systemic), 3–5 weeks (CNS/retina).

## Limitations

Short follow-up; particle yield not clinical-scale; brain editing not reported as a single percentage; single-editor cargo; liver/retina/muscle demonstrations (not tumor).

## Conclusion

eVLPs can deliver editor **protein** (not DNA) transiently at therapeutic in-vivo efficiency with off-target editing near-eliminated — because the editor is present for hours, not weeks. This is the founding demonstration of the transient-delivery-lowers-off-target principle applied to base editors, and the template for the prime-editor and successor eVLP work.
