# Michels 2023 — VivoVec Proof of Concept

The preclinical foundation of the leading clinical in-vivo-CAR-T platform: generating functional CAR-T from unstimulated blood with a built-in safety/expansion switch.

## Design & Methods

UB-VV100 = a cocal-pseudotyped third-generation lentivirus displaying a surface anti-CD3 scFv (T-cell targeting/activation) plus a **rapamycin-activated cytokine receptor (RACR)** for selective CAR-T expansion, carrying a CD19 CAR. Tested on unstimulated human PBMCs, B-ALL/DLBCL patient PBMCs, CD34-humanized mice, Nalm-6 xenografts, and canines.

## Quantitative Results

- Generated **functional anti-CD19 (FMC63) CAR-T from unstimulated PBMCs** — no ex-vivo activation required.
- Dose-dependent B-cell depletion in humanized mice at 0.4, 2.0, and 10.0 ×10⁶ transducing units (TU).
- Tumor clearance in Nalm-6 xenografts at 27–270 ×10⁶ TU; tumor bioluminescence reduction p<0.0001; endogenous B-cell depletion by day 18.
- Rapamycin (10 nM) selectively expanded CAR⁺ cells over 21 days; CAR-negative cells suppressed.
- Biodistribution: transduction "limited to the immune compartment"; RNA-ISH confirmed CD3⁺ T cells (and some mouse CD68⁺ macrophages) transduced. Canine: transduction confined to the lymphoid compartment.

## Limitations

Off-target macrophage transduction observed; xenograft/humanized-mouse models; no nonhuman primate in this paper (that is Nicolai 2024).

## Conclusion

In-vivo CAR-T generation from a single injection is feasible with a targeted lentivirus, and a rapamycin/RACR switch adds selective expansion and a safety lever. This proof of concept, extended to primates by Nicolai 2024, is the platform now in first-in-human trials (Umoja, and the related Interius and Kelonia programs).
