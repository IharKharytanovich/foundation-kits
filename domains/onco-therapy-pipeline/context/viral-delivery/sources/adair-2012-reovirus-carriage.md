# Adair 2012 — Cell Carriage of Oncolytic Reovirus in Patients

The foundational human demonstration that a replication-competent oncolytic virus can survive intravenous delivery by riding blood cells, even in patients who already carry neutralizing antibodies against it.

## Design & Cohort

Window-of-opportunity (neoadjuvant) clinical trial with intensive correlative virology, referred to as REO 013 in the clinical-development literature. **10 patients** with colorectal cancer metastatic to the liver, scheduled for surgical resection, received a single cycle of **intravenous reovirus type 3 Dearing (Reolysin/pelareorep)** before surgery. Virus was then sought in the resected tumor versus adjacent normal liver, and in fractionated blood. (The exact per-cohort TCID₅₀ dose/schedule sits in the paywalled Methods and is not verified to the digit.)

## Methods

Reovirus was localized in tissue by immunohistochemistry, in-situ hybridization, and electron microscopy (viral factories co-stained with tubulin). Replication-competence was tested by recovering infectious virus from fresh tumor and normal-liver tissue. Blood was fractionated into plasma, PBMCs, granulocytes, platelets, and erythrocytes, and each fraction assayed for reovirus genome (two independent primer sets) and for viable cytotoxic virus.

## Quantitative Results

- **Tumor detection:** reovirus protein by IHC positive in **9 of 10** patients, staining greatest in tumor metastases versus surrounding stroma or normal liver.
- **Replication in tumor:** replication-competent virus recovered from fresh tumor in **all 4 of 4** patients with available fresh tissue — and from **none** of their normal-liver samples. Viral factories seen by EM; findings consistent with reovirus-associated apoptosis in 4 patients.
- **Blood-cell carriage:** reovirus genome detected in plasma and in the PBMC, granulocyte, and platelet fractions, but **replication-competent, cytotoxic virus was recovered only from the blood cells, not from plasma** (nor erythrocytes).
- **Antibody status:** **all 10 patients were neutralizing-antibody-positive at baseline**, yet cell-associated virus stayed viable.
- **Temporal window:** virus detectable on blood cells from 1 hour to as late as 5 days post-infusion.

## Limitations

The precise hand-off mechanism from carrier cell to tumor and the therapeutic efficacy of the delivered virus "remain to be determined." Single-arm, small n, correlative endpoints.

## Conclusion

Contrary to mouse models — where pre-existing neutralizing antibodies block IV oncolytic virus outright — human blood cells shield reovirus from circulating antibody and deliver it selectively to tumor after intravenous administration. This is the empirical basis for intravenous (not merely intratumoral) oncolytic-virus dosing and for the carrier-cell bypass strategy.
