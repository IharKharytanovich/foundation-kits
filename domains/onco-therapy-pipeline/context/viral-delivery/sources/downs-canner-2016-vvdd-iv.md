# Downs-Canner 2016 — vvDD IV, the Delivery-Barrier Document

The most explicit clinical documentation of the systemic-delivery wall: intravenous vaccinia is in the blood at minutes, mostly gone by hours, and reaches only a quarter of biopsied tumors.

## Design & Cohort

Phase 1. **11 patients** with advanced solid cancers received a single **intravenous 3×10⁸–3×10⁹ pfu** of double-deleted vaccinia (vvDD, Western Reserve, TK⁻/VGF⁻). Blood PK, tumor-biopsy genome detection, and anti-vaccinia antibody kinetics measured.

## Quantitative Results

- **Blood: 11/11 (100%) genome-positive at 15/30 minutes, but only 4/11 (36%) at 4 hours** — rapid clearance.
- **Of 8 evaluable tumor biopsies, only 2 (25%) had vvDD genomes** (days 8 and 22).
- **>100-fold rise in anti-vaccinia antibody by day 22.**
- No RECIST responses; median OS 4.8 months.
- Replicating virus was also recovered from a healing surgical wound (2.5×10³ pfu) — i.e. not tumor-selective versus inflamed tissue.

## Author-Stated Conclusion & Recommendation

"Most patients appeared to clear the virus quickly, limiting its ability to infect metastases." The authors recommended combining IV oncolytic vaccinia with complement inhibition and checkpoint blockade.

## Limitations

Small n; single dose; the result is specific to a non-blood-stable virus (contrast enadenotucirev's 11/12) and to a virus most adults are immune to via smallpox vaccination.

## Conclusion

Intravenous vaccinia is cleared within hours and reaches only 2/8 tumors, with a >100-fold antibody surge by day 22 — a direct clinical measurement of the systemic-delivery wall (rapid clearance + antibody + non-selectivity). It is the empirical case for the carrier-cell, shielding, and complement-inhibition bypasses.
