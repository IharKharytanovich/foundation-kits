# Evgin 2015 — Complement Dominates Vaccinia Neutralization

Establishes that for oncolytic vaccinia the blood barrier is primarily **complement**, with antibody acting largely by amplifying complement — and that pharmacological complement inhibition is a translatable delivery adjunct.

## Design & Methods

Neutralization assays in vaccinated-human plasma and cynomolgus-macaque serum, with pathway dissection using pathway-specific inhibitors (anti-C1q, anti-Factor B, C5 inhibition) and the compstatin analog CP40. In-vivo delivery tested in rats (with cobra venom factor, CVF, to deplete complement) and in immune macaques (with CP40).

## Quantitative Results

- Vaccinated human plasma killed **up to 99%** of vaccinia infectivity; heat inactivation (which destroys complement) restored only ~20% → **most neutralization is complement-dependent**. CP40 completely blocked antibody-mediated neutralization.
- **Pathway dissection** (fold-increase in recovered titer): anti-C1q **3×**; anti-Factor B **13×**; combined **38×**; C5 inhibition **7×** in naïve but **52×** in immune serum.
- **Rat IV:** complement depletion (CVF) gave **>100-fold** higher blood infectious titer at 10 minutes; intratumoral titer rose **20×** at 24 h and **117×** at 48 h.
- **Macaque:** CP40 in immune animals gave a mean **9.93-fold** (range 5.8–14.8) higher peak titer and extended detectable virus from 10–30 minutes to **2 hours**.
- Pexa-Vec trial sera (NCT01394939) showed complement-dependent neutralization at 2 weeks. Measles and reovirus are also complement-dependent; HSV-1 is not.

## Limitations

Complement inhibition is a systemic intervention with its own infection risks; the study establishes proof-of-mechanism, not a clinical delivery protocol.

## Conclusion

For vaccinia (and measles, reovirus), complement is the dominant early blood inactivator and antibody acts largely by triggering complement — so complement inhibition, not just antibody evasion, is a rational way to widen the systemic-delivery window. It reframes the neutralizing-antibody wall as substantially a complement wall for these virus families.
