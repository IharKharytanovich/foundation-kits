# Khare 2011 — Kupffer Cells Destroy ~90% of an IV Adenovirus Dose

The canonical anchor for hepatosplenic sequestration: the reticuloendothelial system, not the tumor, is where most of an intravenous adenovirus dose ends up.

## Design & Methods

Engineered an Ad5/6 chimera by swapping the Ad5 hexon hypervariable regions (HVRs) for those of Ad6, then compared biodistribution, Kupffer-cell uptake, and Factor X (FX) binding. Models: BALB/c mice and Syrian hamsters, with and without high-dose Kupffer-cell pre-depletion.

## Quantitative Results

- **The canonical figure:** "as much as **90% of an intravenous Ad5 dose is absorbed and destroyed by liver Kupffer cells**."
- The Ad5/6 HVR chimera achieved **>10-fold higher liver transduction** in mice (3-fold in hamster) by evading Kupffer uptake.
- **Kupffer toxicity:** high-dose Ad5 caused a 20-fold drop in F4/80⁺ Kupffer cells at 6 hours; Ad5/6 only 3-fold.
- **FX affinity:** Ad5 hexon K_D = 1.68×10⁻⁹ M versus Ad5/6 = 1.63×10⁻⁸ M (~10× lower FX binding for the chimera).
- **Hexon charge:** Ad5 hexon carries 31 negative / 17 positive residues; Ad6 25 negative / 12 positive (~40% charge reduction) — charge governs both FX binding and Kupffer uptake.

## Limitations

Liver-directed engineering (the goal here was more liver transduction, the inverse of the oncolytic goal), but the ~90% Kupffer-loss figure is the quantitative anchor for the sequestration barrier. Species-dependent (mouse/hamster).

## Conclusion

Up to ~90% of intravenous adenovirus is scavenged by liver Kupffer cells, and this loss is mechanistically tied to hexon HVR charge and Factor X binding — one of three converging mechanisms (Kupffer, platelets, erythrocytes) that each account for ~90% loss of an IV dose and together make naked systemic adenovirus delivery grossly inefficient.
