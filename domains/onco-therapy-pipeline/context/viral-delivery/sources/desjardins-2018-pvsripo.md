# Desjardins 2018 — PVSRIPO's Durable Survival Tail in GBM

A landmark showing that an oncolytic virus can produce a durable long-survivor plateau in glioblastoma even when the median survival barely beats historical control — the "tail," not the median, is the signal.

## Design & Cohort

Phase 1, dose-finding. **61 patients** with recurrent WHO grade IV glioblastoma received **PVSRIPO** (lerapolturev; a live attenuated Sabin type-1 poliovirus with its internal ribosome entry site replaced by that of human rhinovirus type 2, targeting the CD155 receptor overexpressed on tumor and antigen-presenting cells) by intratumoral convection-enhanced delivery across 7 dose levels (10⁷–10¹⁰ TCID₅₀); recommended dose 5.0×10⁷ TCID₅₀ (neurologic dose-limiting toxicity drove de-escalation). Compared with a matched historical control.

## Quantitative Results

- **Median OS 12.5 months** (95% CI 9.9–15.2) versus 11.3 months historical control — the median barely beats control.
- **The signal is the plateau: 24-month OS 21% (95% CI 11–33) and 36-month OS 21%** — a flat tail — versus historical 14% at 24 months and 4% at 36 months (which kept declining).
- Interim cohort (n=24, the Breakthrough-designation basis): 24-month 24%, 36-month 8%.
- Mechanism: CD155-targeted infection of tumor + antigen-presenting cells → sustained inflammatory / type-I-interferon response.

## Limitations

Small phase 1; historical (not randomized) control; neurologic toxicity capped the dose; the durable benefit is confined to a ~21% subset, not the whole population.

## Conclusion

PVSRIPO produces a durable ~21% long-survivor plateau at 24 and 36 months in recurrent glioblastoma — a qualitatively different endpoint from median survival, and the archetype for reading oncolytic-virus benefit as a durable tail. FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation followed; a randomized phase 2 with pembrolizumab (LUMINOS-101) is ongoing.
