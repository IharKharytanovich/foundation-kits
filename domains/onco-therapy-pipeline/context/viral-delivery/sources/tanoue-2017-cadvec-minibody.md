# Tanoue 2017 — Local PD-L1 Minibody Beats Systemic Antibody

The cleanest demonstration that *where* a checkpoint blocker is delivered — not merely that it is present — drives the outcome, and the mechanistic basis for arming viruses with checkpoint payloads.

## Design & Cohort

Binary system: an oncolytic adenovirus (Onc.Ad) plus a helper-dependent adenovirus (HDAd) encoding an anti-PD-L1 minibody (CAd-VEC_PDL1). Tested against PC-3 prostate (n=8, NSG mice) and SiHa cervical (n=5) xenografts, with or without HER2-targeted CAR T cells. Dose 1×10⁸ viral particles intratumorally (some at 1×10⁷).

## Quantitative Results

- **Antibody localization (key finding):** the PD-L1 minibody was detected in tumor lysates at days 3, 7, and 21 but was **NOT detectable in serum** — local delivery with no systemic exposure.
- **Vector amplification** (binary system vs HDAd alone): 4-fold (A549), 15-fold (PC-3), 10-fold (HepG2); 1,000-fold more HDAd copies at 48 hours.
- **Tumor volume:** CAd-VEC_PDL1 alone gave 60% lower tumor volume versus control at day 35 (PC-3).
- **Survival — local vs systemic antibody (key delta):** CAd-VEC_PDL1 + HER2.CAR reached **110 days** median survival versus **59 days** for arms using systemic anti-PD-L1 IgG — roughly 2-fold longer than either single agent (~60 days). SiHa: 42 versus 24 days untreated.
- **T-cell effects:** in-vitro CAR killing +2–3-fold, CAR expansion +5-fold (SiHa); tumor T cells with 30% lower PD-1; residual tumor 50% less PD-L1. **Systemic IgG gave 30–90% lower intratumoral T-cell activity than local delivery.**

## Limitations

Xenograft/CAR-dependent; efficacy largely as a combination with CAR-T; no syngeneic immunocompetent data; absolute intratumoral concentration reported qualitatively.

## Conclusion

An armed oncolytic virus that secretes a checkpoint blocker locally reaches therapeutic intratumoral levels while sparing systemic exposure, and outperforms the same antibody given systemically. This is the empirical foundation of local payload secretion — the reason to put a checkpoint (or cytokine) payload into the virus rather than co-administering the drug.
