# Roulstone 2021 — Neutralizing Antibody Kinetics After IV Reovirus

Quantifies exactly how fast and how large the humoral response to intravenous oncolytic reovirus is — the numbers that define the repeat-dosing window.

## Design & Methods

Pooled analysis of serum antiviral (neutralizing anti-reovirus, NARA) antibody responses across systemic reovirus trials, measured over time by isotype and titer, and stratified by concurrent chemotherapy regimen.

## Quantitative Results

- **Onset:** NARA titers begin rising **5–8 days** after the first reovirus infusion.
- **Peak:** titers most often peak **~15 days** post-infusion.
- **Isotype:** **IgG1-dominant** — mean 10.3 mg/mL at cycle 1 day 8 versus 3.2 mg/mL IgM.
- **Magnitude:** titers rise **>50-fold in nearly all patients** (corroborated by the clinical-development review, which reports >50-fold rise in all but one patient across IV-reovirus trials).
- **Chemotherapy effect:** peak titers were **1–2 logs lower with concurrent gemcitabine** than with other regimens — gemcitabine attenuates the humoral response.
- **Baseline:** all patients had low levels of pre-existing anti-reovirus antibody (ubiquitous virus); precise baseline seropositivity not quantified in this pooled analysis.

## Cross-Trial Corroboration

Reoviral RNA remained detectable in PBMCs by RT-PCR despite the large NARA rise — antibody rise does not fully clear circulating virus/genome. Across the reovirus myeloma and pancreatic datasets, NARA magnitude did **not** track clinical response, consistent with cell-carriage protection.

## Limitations

Pooled/retrospective; baseline seropositivity percentage not quantified; reovirus-specific (kinetics differ by virus family, but the near-universal-seroconversion pattern generalizes to measles and Seneca Valley virus).

## Conclusion

Intravenous oncolytic reovirus reliably induces a strong, rapid, IgG1-dominant humoral response — onset day 5–8, peak ~day 15, >50-fold rise in nearly all patients — which caps repeat systemic dosing but, because carrier cells shield the virus, does not by itself determine efficacy.
