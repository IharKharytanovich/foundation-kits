# Kennedy 2022 — Synthetic RNA Virus, Delivered as LNP-mRNA

The most complete escape from the neutralizing-antibody wall: deliver the oncolytic virus's genome as synthetic RNA in a lipid nanoparticle, so there is no capsid for antibody to recognize.

## Design & Methods

The full genome of Seneca Valley virus (SVV, an ANTXR1/TEM8-tropic oncolytic picornavirus) was synthesized as RNA and formulated in a lipid nanoparticle (LNP), rather than injecting SVV virions. Tested against native SVV virions under neutralizing antisera, and for repeat-dosing tolerability. Models: NCI-H446 small-cell lung cancer and other lines; mice and cynomolgus macaques.

## Quantitative Results

- **Antibody evasion:** synthetic-SVV RNA **retained potency under anti-SVV neutralizing antisera**, while native SVV virions were **completely abrogated** by the same antisera — because the LNP presents no viral capsid.
- **Repeat dosing:** tolerated up to **4 weekly intravenous doses in mice** and **3 biweekly 1 mg/kg doses in cynomolgus macaques**.
- **Replication kinetics:** tumor negative-strand (replicating) RNA appeared by day 3, plateaued by day 7, and was sustained to day 21.
- **Efficacy:** complete regressions of SK-MEL-28 melanoma at 0.05–0.2 mg/kg; roughly doubled survival in orthotopic NCI-H82 (n=15/group). Immune shift: CD8 increase (p=0.0002) and M1/M2 macrophage ratio increase (p=0.0002).

## Limitations

Single-genome (SVV) demonstration; long-term repeat-dosing immunogenicity of the encoded viral proteins (once translated) versus the capsid is a distinct question; LNP itself has biodistribution constraints (the liver default of the nanoparticle platform).

## Conclusion

Delivering an oncolytic-virus genome as LNP-formulated synthetic RNA sidesteps capsid-directed neutralizing antibodies on the very first dose, enabling genuine repeat intravenous dosing — the convergence point between oncolytic virotherapy and the LNP/mRNA delivery platform, and the cleanest form of the virion-shielding bypass.
