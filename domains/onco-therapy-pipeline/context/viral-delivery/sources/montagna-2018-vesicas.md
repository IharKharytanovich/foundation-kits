# Montagna 2018 (VEsiCas) — Genome-Wide Off-Target Collapse

The strongest genome-wide evidence for transient delivery lowering off-target: a vesicle-delivered Cas9 RNP produces one off-target site where plasmid produces 87.

## Design & Methods

VSV-G-enveloped vesicles (VEsiCas) packaging Cas9 ribonucleoprotein, compared with plasmid-encoded Cas9. Off-target editing assessed genome-wide by **GUIDE-seq** and by deep sequencing at VEGFA off-target sites; Cas9 clearance kinetics tracked.

## Quantitative Results

- **GUIDE-seq: plasmid delivery cut 87 off-target sites; VEsiCas detected a single off-target site (87 → 1).**
- **17–22× fewer off-target indels** at VEGFA OT1/OT3; **37× better on/off-target ratio**.
- Cas9 was cleared by ~24 hours (detectable at 6 h, gone over the next 18 h) versus plasmid, which kept accumulating.
- The authors state: "high expression levels of Cas9 for long periods result in high numbers of non-specific cleavages."

## Limitations

In-vitro genome-wide assay; VSV-G immunogenicity on repeat dosing not evaluated; magnitude is locus/guide-dependent.

## Conclusion

Genome-wide, transient vesicle-delivered Cas9 RNP collapses the off-target landscape from 87 sites to 1 relative to plasmid — the clearest whole-genome demonstration that editor exposure time, not intrinsic guide fidelity alone, drives off-target burden. It sits alongside Kim 2014 and Banskota 2022 as the quantitative backbone of the transient-vs-persistent principle, and its VSV-G-vesicle format is a sibling of the engineered-VLP platform.
