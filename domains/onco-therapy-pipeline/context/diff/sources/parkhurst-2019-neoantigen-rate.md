# Parkhurst 2019 — Immunogenic Rate Over All Mutations

The corroborating empirical anchor for the neoantigen wall, using a different denominator than TESLA: the fraction of *all screened somatic mutations* that are T-cell immunogenic.

## Design

**75 patients** with mismatch-repair-proficient/MSS gastrointestinal cancers; somatic mutations screened via tandem minigenes / peptide pools + autologous tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL).

## Results (load-bearing)

- Neoantigen-reactive TIL in **62/75 (83%)** of patients; **124 reactive TIL populations.**
- **1.6% of the gene products encoded by somatic non-synonymous mutations were immunogenic** (denominator = all screened variants, ~73% of variants screened; example colorectal 94/5,833 = 1.6%).
- **99% of neoantigenic determinants were unique** (private to the patient).

Verbatim: *"1.6% of the gene products encoded by somatic non-synonymous mutations were immunogenic."*

## Provenance Distinction

The ~1–2% (Parkhurst, over all screened mutations) and TESLA's 6% (over top-ranked shortlisted candidates) bound the same wall with different denominators. Extending the range: Müller 2023 Immunity (131 patients) found **178/1,781,445 neo-peptides (~0.01%) and 212/46,017 mutations (~0.46%)** immunogenic (ML improved ranking by up to 30%); Ketelaars 2025 found **34/8,103 (0.4%)** predicted neoantigens immunogenic in lower-TMB tumors.

## Limitations

MSS GI cancers (relatively low TMB); functional TIL screen depends on TIL availability. Together with TESLA, this fixes the immunogenicity yield at a low single-digit percent — the empirical reality the prediction models must confront.
