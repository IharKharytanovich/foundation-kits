# NTLA-2001 — Genetic-Branch Worked Case (Liver Editing Bypass)

## Trial Design

Phase 1 open-label, dose-escalation study of NTLA-2001 in patients with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with polyneuropathy (hATTR-PN). NTLA-2001 is a single-dose IV infusion of LNP carrying Cas9 mRNA + a guide RNA targeting the TTR gene in hepatocytes. The goal: knock out TTR production in the liver to eliminate the misfolded transthyretin protein causing systemic amyloid deposition.

Sponsored by Intellia Therapeutics. Initial data (6 patients) published by Gillmore et al. in NEJM 2021; updated data with expanded cohorts presented at medical conferences through 2024.

## Pipeline Trace (Genetic Branch)

1. **Sampling**: patient genotyping confirms pathogenic TTR variant (germline, not somatic — ATTR is a monogenic disease). Blood draw for baseline serum TTR measurement.
2. **Diff**: minimal — the target is a known germline variant in a single gene (TTR). No tumor-normal pairing, no somatic calling, no neoantigen prediction. The "diff" is the molecular diagnosis of ATTR.
3. **Design (genetic branch)**: CRISPR/Cas9 guide RNA targeting TTR exon 1 (designed for allele-agnostic knockout — works across >100 known pathogenic TTR variants). Cas9 mRNA (SpCas9) with modified nucleosides. Guide RNA chemically modified for stability.
4. **Manufacture**: LNP encapsulation of Cas9 mRNA + guide RNA; ionizable lipid formulation (proprietary); manufactured as a fixed product (not patient-specific — same construct for all patients). Standard GMP production, not N-of-1.
5. **Delivery**: single IV infusion; LNP defaults to the liver via ApoE adsorption and LDLR-mediated uptake in hepatocytes. This is the key: the therapy works precisely because the target organ is the liver, and LNP liver tropism is the default — no engineering needed to redirect. The solid-tumor delivery wall does not apply.
6. **Efficacy**: serum TTR reduction as primary pharmacodynamic endpoint; neurological/cardiac outcomes as clinical endpoints.

## Key Results

- **Serum TTR reduction of 87% (0.3 mg/kg dose) and 93% (0.7 mg/kg dose)** at 28 days post-infusion in the initial 6-patient cohort (Gillmore 2021).
- Durable knockdown: TTR levels remained suppressed at >2 years follow-up in updated data (2023-2024 presentations), with mean reductions >90% sustained.
- Dose-dependent editing: higher LNP doses produced deeper TTR knockdown, consistent with dose-response of hepatocyte editing efficiency.
- Safety: transient infusion-related reactions (cytokine release, mild); transient liver enzyme elevations (ALT/AST); no serious adverse events attributed to off-target editing.
- FDA Fast Track designation granted 2022; Phase 2/3 studies ongoing.

## Why This Works (Delivery-Wall Bypass)

NTLA-2001 demonstrates the genetic-branch bypass: when the target organ is the liver, LNP delivery is solved by default. ApoE-mediated LDLR uptake in hepatocytes delivers >80% of IV-administered LNP dose to the liver — the same tropism that is the barrier for solid-tumor delivery becomes the enabler for liver-targeted editing.

This case does not extend to solid-tumor editing. The 0.7% median tumor accumulation of injected nanoparticles (Wilhelm 2016) means that the same LNP platform that achieves 93% TTR knockdown in the liver would deliver a negligible editing dose to a solid tumor. NTLA-2001 is the proof that in-vivo gene editing works clinically — but only where the delivery wall does not apply.

The case also illustrates the difference between personalized and fixed therapies: NTLA-2001 is a fixed product (same construct for all patients), so the manufacturing and regulatory path is standard GMP, not N-of-1. The CMC wall that constrains personalized therapies does not apply here.

## Source Quality

Phase 1 dose-escalation; initial publication in NEJM (6 patients, peer-reviewed); updated data in conference presentations (expanded cohorts, >2 year follow-up). Confidence: high for TTR knockdown (clear dose-response, durable), moderate for long-term safety (ongoing monitoring).
