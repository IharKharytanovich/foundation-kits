# Oncolytic Virus Platforms in Oncology (2024–2025)

## Approved: T-VEC (Imlygic)

- **Talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC)**: first oncolytic virus approved (FDA 2015, EMA 2016) for unresectable stage IIIB–IV melanoma. Modified HSV-1 armed with GM-CSF. Intratumoral injection only.
- **ORR**: 26.4% (vs 5.7% for GM-CSF alone); durable response rate 16.3% (OPTiM trial, Andtbacka et al., JCO 2015).
- **Limitation**: intratumoral injection limits applicability to accessible lesions; systemic anti-tumor effect (abscopal) observed but inconsistent. T-VEC failed to improve OS significantly when combined with pembrolizumab in MASTERKEY-265 Phase 3 (reported 2023).
- **Commercial status**: withdrawn from EU market (2023) due to low uptake; remains available in the US.

## Next-Generation: Armed and Engineered Platforms

### Replimune Pipeline (HSV-based)

- **RP1 (vusolimogene oraplasmid)**: HSV-1 armed with GM-CSF and an anti-CTLA-4 antibody-like molecule (GALV-GP R-). Phase 2 IGNYTE trial in anti-PD-1-failed melanoma: ORR 33.1% (including uninjected lesions), with CRs in ~10%. Phase 3 initiated.
- **RP2**: HSV-1 armed with anti-CTLA-4 and a bispecific anti-PD-1/anti-CD28 molecule. Phase 1/2 showed ORR 37% in anti-PD-1-refractory melanoma (n=35).
- **RP3**: next-generation armed HSV carrying IL-12, anti-CTLA-4, and a 4-1BB agonist. Preclinical/early Phase 1.

### CG0070 (Adenovirus-based)

- **CG Oncology CG0070**: oncolytic adenovirus with GM-CSF, selectively replicating in Rb-pathway-deficient cells. Phase 3 BOND-003 in BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC).
- **Phase 2 data**: CR rate 75% at 6 months in high-risk NMIBC (intravesical delivery — bladder is an accessible cavity, not a solid-tumor delivery problem).

### Other Platforms

- **Teserpaturev (G47Δ)**: triple-mutated HSV-1, conditionally approved in Japan (2021) for malignant glioma (intratumoral via stereotactic injection).
- **LOTVS (LOAd703)**: adenovirus armed with TMZ-CD40L and 4-1BBL, Phase 1/2 in pancreatic cancer + gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel.
- **Pelareorep**: unmodified reovirus, Phase 2 in breast cancer + paclitaxel (BRACELET-1).

## Engineered Selectivity Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Example | Selectivity basis |
|---|---|---|
| Tumor-suppressor-pathway deletion | CG0070 (Rb-deficient cells) | Virus replicates only in cells with defective Rb pathway |
| Attenuated neurovirulence | T-VEC, G47Δ (ICP34.5 deletion) | Cannot replicate in normal neurons/tissue |
| Tumor-specific promoter | Various preclinical | Transgene/replication driven by hTERT, survivin, etc. |
| MicroRNA detargeting | Preclinical | miR-122/miR-199 target sites silence replication in liver/normal tissue |

## Key Limitations

1. **Delivery**: all approved and late-clinical OVs require intratumoral or intracavitary injection. Systemic delivery faces neutralizing antibodies (50–90% seroprevalence for HSV/adenovirus), complement inactivation, and hepatic sequestration.
2. **Manufacturing**: large-scale production of replication-competent viruses under GMP is complex; batch-to-batch titer variability; cold-chain requirements.
3. **Selectivity vs potency tradeoff**: heavy attenuation (multiple gene deletions) improves safety but reduces replicative fitness and oncolytic potency.

## Significance for the Pipeline

Oncolytic viruses are a genetic-branch modality that bypasses the systemic delivery wall via local injection. They are NOT personalized to the patient's mutations — selectivity is to the tumor phenotype (Rb-deficient, p53-mutant), not the patient's unique mutation profile. For the pipeline, OVs represent a parallel therapeutic option in the genetic branch, complementary to but distinct from the allele-specific approaches (CRISPR, ASO/siRNA).
