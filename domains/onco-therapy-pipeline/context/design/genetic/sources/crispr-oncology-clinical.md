# CRISPR and Base Editing in Oncology: Clinical State (2024–2025)

## Ex Vivo Editing — The Dominant Clinical Path

All clinically advanced CRISPR oncology programs as of 2025 are **ex vivo**: cells are extracted, edited outside the body, expanded, and re-infused. This sidesteps the delivery wall entirely.

### Allogeneic CAR-T with CRISPR Knockout

- **CTX110 (CRISPR Therapeutics)**: allogeneic anti-CD19 CAR-T with CRISPR knockout of TRAC (to prevent GvHD) and beta-2-microglobulin (to evade host rejection). Phase 1 data (ASH 2022): ORR 67% in r/r B-ALL, 38% in r/r DLBCL. Manufacturing from a healthy donor bank eliminates patient-specific manufacturing delay — off-the-shelf, <5 days from order to infusion.
- **CTX130**: allogeneic anti-CD70 CAR-T for renal cell carcinoma and T-cell lymphoma. Phase 1 ongoing.
- **CARBON (Caribou Biosciences)**: chRDNA-guided editing (Cas12a) for allogeneic CAR-T (CB-010, anti-CD19; CB-011, anti-BCMA). PD-1 knockout integrated into the construct.

### Base-Edited CAR-T

- **BEAM-201 (Beam Therapeutics)**: base-edited allogeneic anti-CD7 CAR-T for T-ALL. Four simultaneous base edits (CD7 knockout, TRAC knockout, CD52 knockout for alemtuzumab resistance, PD-1 silencing) without double-strand breaks — reduced translocation risk versus Cas9.
- **BE-CAR7 (UCL/Great Ormond Street)**: base-edited anti-CD7 CAR-T. First patient treated in 2022 (Alyssa, 13-year-old T-ALL); complete remission reported.

### First In-Human CRISPR Oncology Trial

- **UPenn study (Stadtmauer et al., Science 2020)**: first CRISPR-edited cells infused into cancer patients (3 patients with refractory cancers). Ex vivo knockout of PD-1 (PDCD1), TRAC, TRBC in autologous T cells. Demonstrated safety and persistence of edited cells for ≥9 months, but no objective tumor responses.

## In Vivo Editing — Preclinical for Solid Tumors

- **Intellia NTLA-2001**: in vivo LNP-delivered CRISPR for transthyretin amyloidosis (liver target). Phase 1 showed >90% TTR reduction (Gillmore et al., NEJM 2021). This is the most advanced in vivo CRISPR program, but it targets the liver — NOT a solid tumor.
- **Verve VERVE-101/VERVE-102**: in vivo base editing targeting PCSK9 (liver) for cardiovascular disease. Phase 1 ongoing.
- **Solid tumor in vivo editing**: no human trial as of 2025. LNP defaults to liver; achieving editing in disseminated solid tumor cells requires solving the delivery wall (see delivery cluster). Intratumoral injection of CRISPR RNPs has been demonstrated in mouse models but not in human trials.

## Key Numbers

| Application | Clinical stage | Editing efficiency | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ex vivo CAR-T (CRISPR knockout) | Phase 1–2 | >90% knockout at target locus | N/A (ex vivo) |
| Ex vivo CAR-T (base editing) | Phase 1 | >90% C→T conversion at target | N/A (ex vivo) |
| In vivo liver editing (NTLA-2001) | Phase 1 | >90% protein knockdown | LNP → liver |
| In vivo solid tumor editing | Preclinical | Variable | Unsolved |

## Significance for the Pipeline

CRISPR's oncology contribution is almost entirely ex vivo: engineering better immune cells (allogeneic CAR-T, PD-1 knockout) rather than directly editing tumor cells. The genetic branch of the design stage works for creating immune effectors and for liver-reachable targets; it does not yet work for direct in-vivo editing of solid tumors.
