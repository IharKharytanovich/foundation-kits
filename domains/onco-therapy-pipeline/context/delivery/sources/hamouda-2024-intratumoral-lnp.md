# Intratumoral LNP-mRNA — 83–86% Complete Regression in Mice

Hamouda et al. 2024 demonstrated that direct intratumoral injection of LNP-encapsulated mRNA encoding immunostimulatory cytokines achieved high complete regression rates in multiple syngeneic mouse solid tumor models.

## Key Findings

**Complete regression rates: 83–86%.** In CT26 (colorectal) and B16-F10 (melanoma) syngeneic mouse models, intratumoral injection of LNP-mRNA encoding a combination of IL-12, IL-27, and GM-CSF achieved complete tumor regression in 83–86% of treated animals. Responses were durable, with no tumor recurrence observed during 60-day follow-up in complete responders.

**Abscopal effects.** In bilateral tumor models (treated primary + untreated contralateral), intratumoral treatment of one tumor led to regression or growth arrest of the untreated distal tumor in approximately 40–50% of animals, indicating systemic immune activation triggered by local treatment.

**Mechanism: innate + adaptive immunity.** The injected mRNA was transiently expressed by tumor-associated macrophages and dendritic cells, converting the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) into an inflamed state. Depletion studies confirmed that CD8+ T cells were essential for tumor rejection, while NK cells contributed to early tumor control.

**Bypass of systemic delivery barriers.** By injecting directly into the tumor, the approach bypasses the 0.7% ID systemic delivery barrier entirely. Local LNP concentration within the tumor is orders of magnitude higher than achievable via systemic injection (estimated 100–1000× higher local availability).

**Combination with checkpoint inhibitors.** Intratumoral LNP-mRNA combined with systemic anti-PD-1 improved the complete regression rate to >90% and enhanced abscopal responses to >60% in bilateral models.

## Limitations

The approach is limited to accessible tumors (subcutaneous models in mice; clinically, superficial or image-guided-accessible tumors). Deep visceral tumors and disseminated metastases cannot be directly injected. Heterogeneous intratumoral distribution is a recognized challenge — LNP injected into one region of a large tumor may not reach distant tumor margins.

## Impact

This study is a key data point for the bypass strategy: routing around the systemic delivery wall by choosing a local injection path. The 83–86% CR rate compares favorably with the best systemic immunotherapy results and demonstrates that LNP-mRNA platforms can generate potent anti-tumor immunity when the delivery barrier is removed from the equation.
