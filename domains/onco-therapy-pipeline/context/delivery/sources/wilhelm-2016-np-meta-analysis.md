# Nanoparticle Delivery to Tumours — The 0.7% Median

Wilhelm et al. conducted a meta-analysis of 232 datasets from 117 published studies (2005–2015) examining nanoparticle delivery to solid tumors in animal models.

## Key Findings

**Median delivery efficiency: 0.7% of injected dose (%ID).** Across all nanoparticle types (gold, silica, polymeric, lipid, iron oxide, quantum dots), the median fraction of administered nanoparticles that accumulated in the tumor was 0.7% ID. The interquartile range spanned roughly 0.3–2.0% ID.

**The EPR effect is far weaker than assumed.** The Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect — the premise that nanoparticles passively accumulate in tumors due to leaky vasculature and poor lymphatic drainage — accounted for delivery efficiencies well below the 5–10% commonly assumed in preclinical literature. Active targeting (surface ligands) improved median accumulation only modestly, from 0.6% to 0.9% ID.

**Organ clearance dominates.** The liver and spleen captured the majority of injected dose (typically 30–99% combined), driven by mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS) uptake. This first-pass clearance fundamentally limits the dose available for tumor accumulation.

**Inorganic nanoparticles outperformed organic.** Inorganic nanoparticles showed slightly higher tumor accumulation (median ~0.8% ID) compared to organic formulations (median ~0.6% ID), likely reflecting their smaller size and surface characteristics.

**Mouse-to-human translation gap.** All data came from mouse xenograft models. Human tumors have lower vascular density, higher interstitial fluid pressure, and orders-of-magnitude lower blood flow per gram than mouse xenografts, suggesting that the 0.7% figure likely overestimates human delivery efficiency.

## Impact

This meta-analysis fundamentally challenged the field's optimism about passive nanoparticle targeting via EPR. It established 0.7% ID as the empirical baseline that any delivery strategy must be benchmarked against — a figure that the existing `delivery-wall.md` uses as the binding constraint of the pipeline.
