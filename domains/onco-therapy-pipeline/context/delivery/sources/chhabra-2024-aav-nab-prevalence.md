# Global Seroprevalence of Anti-AAV Neutralizing Antibodies

Chhabra et al. 2024 conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of anti-AAV neutralizing antibody (NAb) seroprevalence data spanning multiple serotypes, geographic regions, and assay methodologies.

## Key Findings

**Overall seroprevalence: 55–95% across serotypes.** The proportion of adults with pre-existing NAbs against commonly used AAV serotypes ranged from approximately 55% (AAV5, the least immunogenic clinically used serotype) to over 95% (AAV2, the most common natural infection). AAV8 and AAV9, widely used in clinical gene therapy, showed intermediate seroprevalence of 60–80%.

**Geographic variation.** Seroprevalence was highest in sub-Saharan Africa and South/Southeast Asia (often >80% for AAV2), intermediate in Europe and North America (50–70% for AAV2), and lowest in some East Asian populations — though no region showed seroprevalence below 40% for the most common serotypes.

**Cross-reactivity between serotypes.** Individuals seropositive for one AAV serotype frequently showed cross-reactive NAbs against other serotypes, reflecting conserved capsid epitopes. This cross-reactivity narrows the pool of eligible patients for any AAV-based therapy and essentially precludes serial treatment with different serotypes.

**Dose-dependent neutralization.** Even low-titer NAbs (1:5–1:20) were sufficient to block transduction at clinically relevant AAV doses (10^13–10^14 vg/kg). The effective neutralization threshold is much lower than for many viral vaccines, meaning that most seropositive individuals are ineligible for systemic AAV therapy.

**Implications for gene editing delivery.** AAV-delivered gene editors (e.g., base editors, prime editors packaged in dual-AAV systems) face this immunological barrier on top of their payload size limitation (~4.7 kb per vector). Pre-screening and immunodepletion strategies exist but add cost and complexity and remain unvalidated in pivotal trials.

## Impact

This paper quantifies the immunological wall that AAV delivery faces — a majority of the adult population carries NAbs that neutralize the most commonly used gene therapy vectors. Combined with the inability to redose (secondary immune response is faster and stronger), this makes AAV a poor choice for therapies requiring repeated administration, such as personalized cancer treatments where the target antigen landscape evolves.
