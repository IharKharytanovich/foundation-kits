# NTLA-2001 — The First In-Vivo CRISPR Therapy

NTLA-2001 (Intellia Therapeutics) is a lipid nanoparticle (LNP) encapsulating Cas9 mRNA and a guide RNA targeting the TTR gene in hepatocytes. It is the first systemically administered CRISPR-Cas9 therapy to demonstrate gene editing in humans.

## Key Findings

**Proof of in-vivo CRISPR editing in humans.** In the landmark 2021 NEJM publication, a single IV infusion of NTLA-2001 at the 0.3 mg/kg dose reduced serum TTR protein by a mean of 87% at Day 28 in patients with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR). This was the first clinical demonstration that LNP-delivered CRISPR could edit a gene in the human body.

**Dose-dependent knockdown.** At the lower dose (0.1 mg/kg), mean TTR reduction was 52%. At 0.3 mg/kg, mean reduction was 87%. Updated Phase 1/2 data presented through 2024–2025 showed durable TTR reduction (>90% at 0.3 mg/kg) maintained through 2+ years of follow-up, consistent with permanent gene disruption in long-lived hepatocytes.

**Safety profile.** Infusion-related reactions were mild to moderate and manageable with premedication. No dose-limiting toxicities were reported. Off-target editing analysis (GUIDE-seq, rhAmpSeq) showed no detectable off-target edits above background at nominated sites.

**Liver targeting is the feature.** NTLA-2001 exploits LNP's default hepatic tropism (ApoE adsorption → LDLR-mediated uptake). For liver-expressed targets like TTR, the LNP's natural biodistribution is an advantage — no active targeting is required. This is the paradigm case of the bypass strategy: choosing a target that the delivery platform already reaches efficiently.

**Implications for extrahepatic targets.** While NTLA-2001 validates in-vivo LNP-CRISPR, its success is specific to the liver. The same LNP formulation does not meaningfully transfect non-hepatic tissues (lung, spleen, muscle, or tumor). Adapting LNP-CRISPR for solid tumor applications would require either active retargeting of the LNP (an unsolved problem at clinical scale) or choosing targets accessible via the liver (e.g., circulating proteins, hepatocyte-expressed antigens).

## Impact

NTLA-2001 established that LNP-delivered in-vivo gene editing is clinically viable, safe, and durable in the liver. It anchors the delivery bypass strategy: for any indication where the therapeutic target is hepatic (or where programming liver-derived products is sufficient), LNP-CRISPR works today. For solid tumors, NTLA-2001 is a proof of principle that the editing machinery works — the remaining problem is getting the LNP to the tumor instead of the liver.
