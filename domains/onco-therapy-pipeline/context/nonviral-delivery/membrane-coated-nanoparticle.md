# Cell-Membrane-Coated Nanoparticles

[[membrane-coated-nanoparticle]] wraps a synthetic core (usually PLGA) in a natural plasma membrane so the particle inherits that cell's surface identity — the nanoparticle analogue of the viral [[ov-virion-shielding]] strategy, and a form of [[delivery-bypass]]. The membrane source determines the benefit.

## Red-Blood-Cell Membranes — Stealth and Circulation

RBC-membrane coating carries CD47 (the "don't-eat-me" signal) and extends nanoparticle circulation half-life to 39.6 hours versus 15.8 hours for PEGylated particles (~2.5×). It is the canonical long-circulation stealth layer, onto which targeting membranes can be added.

## Cancer-Cell Membranes — Homotypic Self-Targeting

Coating a particle in the membrane of the tumor cell it is meant to treat gives homotypic self-targeting via surface adhesion molecules. A HepG2-membrane-coated doxorubicin particle showed 4–5× higher uptake in HepG2 versus normal liver cells, ~75% reduction in macrophage internalization, and ~90% tumor-volume reduction in xenografts, with pH-responsive drug release. Cancer-membrane paclitaxel particles gave 6.5× fewer metastatic nodules in a breast model.

## Platelet Membranes — Tumor Retention

Platelet-membrane particles circulate ~30 hours and retain >2× the tumor drug concentration of uncoated particles at 48 hours, inheriting the CD47-SIRPα self-signal; a platelet-membrane siRNA carrier showed 6× higher tumor-cell uptake than an RBC-coated control.

## The Translational Gap

The gains in half-life, tumor accumulation, and selectivity are reproducible — but **entirely preclinical**. No cell-membrane-coated nanoparticle has an approved oncology indication or a reported phase-3 readout; the obstacle is GMP-scale manufacturing and batch consistency of biological membranes. And, like exosomes, these particles improve on the nanoparticles they cloak but remain diffusion-limited — they do not by themselves penetrate a hypoxic tumor core or overcome interstitial pressure.
