# Breda 2023 — In-Vivo HSC Editing via Anti-CD117 LNP

Demonstrates a full in-vivo hematopoietic-stem-cell gene-therapy stack — targeted editing plus antibody-based conditioning — while quantifying the residual liver off-target that antibody conjugation does not eliminate.

## Design & Model

Anti-CD117 (c-kit)-conjugated LNP delivering Cre, Cas9, or adenine base editor (ABE) mRNA to hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), plus a PLK1/PUMA mRNA payload for non-genotoxic conditioning (killing endogenous HSCs to open the niche). Mouse models with transplant chimera confirmation; human sickle-cell cells in vitro.

## Quantitative Results

- **Long-term HSC (SLAM) editing 95%** at 0.1 and 1 µg Cre mRNA (versus 13.5–20% with IgG control).
- RBC/leukocyte editing ≥99% at 0.05–1 µg; 91.8% even at 0.01 µg.
- In-vivo SLAM LT-HSC editing 55%±10 (versus 9%±2.3 control); durable in secondary-transplant chimeras (52% versus 19%).
- **Base editing (ABE) up to 88%** at 10 pg/cell; near-complete correction of hematopoietic sickle cells (human, in vitro).
- Conditioning: anti-CD117/LNP-PUMA at 0.05 mg/kg cut HSC frequency 71%/58%, enabling donor engraftment (25% chimerism) without genotoxic busulfan or irradiation.
- **Off-target (important):** 76–79% of liver cells edited at the 5 µg dose — c-kit is not fully HSC-exclusive.

## Limitations

Mouse in-vivo (human cells in vitro); substantial hepatic off-target editing persists despite antibody targeting.

## Conclusion

Near-complete in-vivo HSC editing plus non-genotoxic antibody-based conditioning is a landmark for in-vivo gene therapy — but the 76–79% liver off-target editing is the honest counterpoint: ligand conjugation mitigates, but does not erase, the LNP liver-default. Motivated clinical in-vivo HSC programs (Beam Therapeutics).
