# Toso 2002 — Salmonella VNP20009 in Patients

The benchmark clinical Salmonella trial and the essential lesson that spectacular preclinical tumor selectivity does not guarantee human systemic colonization.

## Design & Cohort

Phase 1. 25 patients (24 metastatic melanoma, 1 renal cell). Intravenous bolus of attenuated Salmonella typhimurium VNP20009, dose 10⁶ → 10⁹ cfu/m²; maximum tolerated dose 3×10⁸ cfu/m².

## Quantitative Results

- **Tumor colonization: only 3/25** (focal): 2 at 10⁹ and 1 at 3×10⁸ cfu/m².
- **Objective responses: none**, including the colonized-tumor patients.
- Dose-limiting toxicities at 10⁹: thrombocytopenia, anemia, persistent bacteremia, hyperbilirubinemia, gastrointestinal toxicity; dose-related rises in IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-12.

## The Translational Gap

Preclinical Salmonella achieves tumor-to-normal accumulation ratios above 1000:1 — a selectivity that **did not translate** to reliable human systemic colonization here, because attenuation for safety reduced colonization efficiency and the intravenous route hit the cytokine-toxicity ceiling before reaching a colonizing dose. Successor strategies (macrophage-shuttled VNP20009, oxytolerant strains) are preclinical.

## Conclusion

Systemic (intravenous) attenuated Salmonella was safely dosed but colonized only 3/25 tumors with no responses — the defining evidence that bacterial tumor tropism, however extreme in mice, faces a safety-versus-colonization trade-off in humans that favors the intratumoral route (as with Clostridium novyi-NT) over systemic delivery.
