# Sonabend 2023 — SonoCloud-9 BBB Opening in Glioblastoma

The headline clinical demonstration that a skull-implanted ultrasound device repeatedly and safely opens the blood-brain barrier to raise brain-tumor drug concentration.

## Design & Cohort

Phase 1 dose-escalation. 17 recurrent glioblastoma patients (median age 57). A nine-emitter implantable low-intensity-pulsed-ultrasound device plus intravenous microbubbles, combined with IV albumin-bound paclitaxel.

## Quantitative Results

- **Paclitaxel concentration 3.7× higher** in sonicated versus non-sonicated peritumoral brain (0.139 versus 0.037 µM); a linked pharmacokinetic study showed **carboplatin 5.9× higher** (5.878 versus 0.991 µM).
- **BBB-opening volume: median 11.9 mL** (range 3.5–20.9 mL).
- 68 total treatment cycles (median 3 per patient); sonication ~4.5 minutes per session.
- Median PFS 2.9 months; median OS 11 months.
- Safety: two reversible encephalopathy events (one grade 3, one grade 2) at 260 mg/m²; anemia, headache, leukopenia each ~88%. Procedure safe and repeatable.

## Limitations

Small n, single-arm, not powered for efficacy; pharmacokinetic sampling from resected tissue only.

## Conclusion

A skull-implanted ultrasound device can repeatedly and safely open the blood-brain barrier to raise brain-tumor drug concentration several-fold — the human proof that focused ultrasound converts the blood-brain-barrier delivery problem into a local-concentration problem. Companion work (Carpentier 2024, Nat Commun) confirmed the 5.9× carboplatin gain and ~90% BBB disruption; the SONOBIRD phase 3 tests whether the pharmacokinetic gain yields a survival benefit.
