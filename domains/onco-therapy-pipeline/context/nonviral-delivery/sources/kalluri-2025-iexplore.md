# Kalluri 2025 (iEXPLORE) — First-in-Human iExosomes

The clinical readout of the iExosome program: a genuine safety and target-engagement milestone for exosome-based RNAi, tempered by absent monotherapy activity.

## Design & Cohort

First-in-human, open-label, single-center. Phase Ia = 3+3 dose escalation, 10 evaluable patients; Phase Ib = accelerated titration, 3 patients with pre/post biopsies. Population: metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma after standard-of-care failure. iExosomes (mesenchymal-cell exosomes loaded with KRAS-G12D siRNA) infused three times over two weeks per cycle.

## Quantitative Results

- **Safety:** escalated to a maximum 4.8 mg siRNA per infusion; **no dose-limiting toxicities; no maximum tolerated dose reached**; no treatment-related adverse events in Phase Ib. Preclinical biodistribution in mice and rhesus macaques showed pancreatic distribution with negligible toxicity.
- **Target engagement:** several patients showed reduced KRAS-G12D mutant allele fraction, downregulated phospho-ERK, reduced PanCK⁺ tumor cells, and increased intratumoral CD8⁺ T cells post-treatment.
- **Efficacy: no objective responses.** Stable disease in some patients (one up to 6 cycles).
- **Combination rationale:** preclinically iExosomes upregulated tumor Fas receptor enabling CD8 apoptosis and synergized with anti-CTLA-4 (not anti-PD-1).

## Limitations

Tiny n (13 total), heavily pretreated, single-arm; benefit signal is pharmacodynamic/immunologic only; no efficacy endpoint met.

## Conclusion

iExosomes are safe and target-engaging in humans — a milestone for extracellular-vesicle-based RNAi — but monotherapy activity is absent, positioning the platform as a delivery chassis for checkpoint-combination regimens rather than a standalone systemic-delivery solution.
