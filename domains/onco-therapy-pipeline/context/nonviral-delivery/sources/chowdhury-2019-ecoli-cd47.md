# Chowdhury 2019 — Programmable E. coli as a Living Drug Factory

The strongest mechanistic case for bacteria as self-amplifying, tumor-restricted immune-agonist factories.

## Design & Models

Non-pathogenic E. coli engineered with a synchronized lysis circuit (SLC) — quorum-sensing (AHL)-triggered periodic self-lysis that releases an anti-CD47 nanobody (CD47nb) in the tumor. Models: A20 lymphoma, 4T1 triple-negative breast, B16-F10 melanoma (BALB/c and C57BL/6).

## Quantitative Results

- CD47nb affinity ~200× higher than a commercial anti-CD47 antibody; +90% macrophage phagocytosis of A20 in vitro.
- A20 (10⁷ CFU intratumoral every 3–4 days ×4): **complete regression ~day 10, ~80% survival past 90 days**; liver metastases rare at day 30.
- **Abscopal effect:** unilateral injection slowed untreated contralateral tumors (control circuit had none); bacteria stayed confined to the injected lesion (no signal above ~10³ CFU/g in liver/spleen/kidney).
- **Immune memory:** survivors resisted rechallenge at day 90; increased tumor-antigen-specific IFN-γ⁺/granzyme-B⁺ CD8 T cells; declined SIRPα⁺ macrophages.

## Limitations

Preclinical; intratumoral primary route; this specific construct is a delivery + immune-agonist chassis, not yet the clinical strain.

## Conclusion

Engineered bacteria can function as self-amplifying, tumor-restricted immune-agonist factories that produce durable, systemic, memory-forming responses from a confined intratumoral inoculum — the strongest demonstration of the "living drug factory" concept and the unique bacterial advantage of payload self-amplification that fixed-dose nanoparticles cannot match.
