# Zhang 2019 — Homotypic Cancer-Membrane Nanoparticles

Quantifies the two benefits of cancer-cell-membrane coating — homotypic self-targeting and macrophage evasion — in a hepatocellular-carcinoma model.

## Design & Model

HepG2-cancer-cell-membrane-coated PLGA nanoparticles (HepM-PLGA) loaded with doxorubicin. BALB/c nude mice, HepG2 subcutaneous xenografts (3×10⁶ cells; 100–200 mm³ at start); doxorubicin 2.5 mg/kg every other day.

## Quantitative Results

- **Homotypic selectivity:** 4–5× higher uptake in HepG2 versus L02 normal liver; near-exclusive uptake by source HepG2 versus BGC-823/HeLa/MCF-7.
- **Immune evasion:** ~75% reduction in macrophage internalization versus bare PLGA.
- **Drug loading:** 38.88 µg doxorubicin/mg; pH-responsive release (<38% at pH 7.4, ~70% at pH 6.8).
- **Efficacy:** ~90% tumor-volume reduction (Dox-HepM-PLGA) versus 2× growth in free-Dox-PLGA; HepG2 viability 24% versus 87% in normal L02.

## Limitations

Xenograft (immunodeficient) — cannot test immune-mediated effects; homotypic mechanism strongest for the matched cell line; preclinical only.

## Conclusion

Cancer-cell-membrane coating delivers quantifiable self-targeting (4–5×), stealth (~75% less macrophage uptake), and efficacy (~90% tumor reduction) — a reproducible biomimetic delivery platform, but one that (like all cell-membrane-coated nanoparticles) remains preclinical with no clinical oncology endpoint and does not by itself penetrate a hypoxic tumor core.
