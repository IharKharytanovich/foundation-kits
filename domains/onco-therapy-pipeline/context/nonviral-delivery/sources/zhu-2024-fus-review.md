# Zhu 2024 — Systematic Review of FUS BBB Clinical Trials

Aggregates the human evidence for focused-ultrasound blood-brain-barrier opening, providing the cross-trial anchor for the drug-concentration gains.

## Scope

Systematic review of 11 studies across 6 centers, covering 61 patients in 7 trials (mostly phase 0/1) of focused ultrasound + microbubbles for brain-tumor drug delivery.

## Quantitative Results

- **Documented concentration gains:** paclitaxel 3.7×, carboplatin 5.9×, temozolomide 7.7×; single-patient chemotherapy enhancements of 47% and 671%.
- **Large-molecule proof:** Meng et al. (NCT03714243, n=4) reported a **450% increase in trastuzumab (HER2 antibody) uptake** — key evidence that antibodies, not just small molecules, can be delivered.
- Mainprize et al. (NCT02343991, n=5): first-in-human feasibility, 35% contrast enhancement. Park et al. (NCT03712293, n=6): temozolomide delivery feasible.
- **Preclinical benchmark (companion):** focused ultrasound raised agent delivery an average 4.2-fold in glioma models.
- 24 ongoing trials identified, including 2 phase 3 (enrollment up to 560).

## Safety

Transient headache 71%; deficits resolving 1–48 hours with steroids; some grade 3–4 hematologic events.

## Limitations

Aggregates small, heterogeneous phase-0/1 trials; concentration gains vary by agent and device; no phase-3 survival readout yet.

## Conclusion

Across 61 patients and multiple centers, focused ultrasound + microbubbles reproducibly raises brain-tumor drug concentration several-fold — including a ~450% gain for a therapeutic antibody — establishing the platform as the strongest-evidenced physical bypass of the blood-brain barrier and the basis for the ongoing phase-3 trials.
