# Janku 2021 — Clostridium novyi-NT in Patients

Proof that an obligate anaerobe can selectively germinate and lyse the hypoxic-necrotic tumor core in humans — with the infection toxicity that constrains it.

## Design & Cohort

Phase 1, dose escalation. 24 enrolled (22 evaluable), treatment-refractory advanced solid tumors. Single intratumoral injection of Clostridium novyi-NT spores, 1×10⁴ → 3×10⁶ spores; maximum tolerated dose 1×10⁶ spores.

## Quantitative Results

- **Germination (target engagement): 10/24 (42%)** had spore germination producing tumor-confined lysis.
- **Injected-tumor shrinkage: 9/22 (41%)**; 86% stable disease as best response.
- **Toxicity:** 3 dose-limiting toxicities — grade 4 sepsis (n=2), grade 4 gas gangrene (n=1), all in tumors >8 cm; additional grade ≥3 abscess/soft-tissue infections.

## Limitations

Local (intratumoral) not systemic; germination in fewer than half of patients; serious infectious toxicity gated by tumor size; no systemic/abscopal efficacy quantified.

## Conclusion

An anaerobe can selectively lyse the hypoxic tumor core in humans — feasible and manageable, but germination reliability and infection risk constrain it. It is the clinical proof that bacteria uniquely exploit the tumor hypoxia that defeats other delivery methods, complementing the Salmonella (systemic) and programmable-E. coli (immune-factory) approaches.
