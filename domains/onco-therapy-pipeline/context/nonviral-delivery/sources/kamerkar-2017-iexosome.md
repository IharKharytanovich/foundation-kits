# Kamerkar 2017 — iExosomes for KRAS-G12D siRNA

The mechanistic foundation of the iExosome clinical program: exosomes as a CD47-shielded, macropinocytosis-exploiting RNAi carrier that outperforms liposomes in pancreatic cancer.

## Design & Models

Mesenchymal/fibroblast-derived exosomes electroporated with siRNA or shRNA against KRAS-G12D ("iExosomes"), compared with iLiposomes. Tested across Panc-1 orthotopic (nude), KTC and KPC genetically engineered mice, and KPC689 orthotopic (immunocompetent). Loading ~10⁹ exosomes with 1 µg siRNA.

## Quantitative & Mechanistic Results

- **CD47-dependent circulation:** CD47-high exosomes showed higher retention; CD47-knockout diminished it; anti-CD47 antibody increased circulating monocyte capture — CD47 shields from monocyte clearance.
- **Macropinocytosis entry:** a macropinocytosis inhibitor reduced exosome (not liposome) uptake; high-macropinocytosis Panc-1 took up more than BxPC-3.
- **Survival:** Panc-1 orthotopic — all iExosome-treated animals alive at day 87 (all controls dead), tumors "nearly undetectable," persisting past 200 days. KTC/KPC models showed significant survival extension. In immunocompetent KPC689, **CD47-knockout exosomes failed** — efficacy is CD47-dependent.

## Limitations

All preclinical; benefit contingent on high macropinocytosis (KRAS-driven) and CD47; single-target siRNA; no human data in this paper.

## Conclusion

Exosomes are a CD47-shielded, macropinocytosis-exploiting siRNA carrier that reached "nearly undetectable" tumor in mice — the mechanistic basis for the iEXPLORE first-in-human trial, and a demonstration that biological carriers exploit tumor biology (CD47 tolerance, KRAS macropinocytosis) that synthetic nanoparticles cannot easily mimic.
