# Grosskopf/Appel 2022 — CAR-T Stimulatory Hydrogel Niche

Demonstrates that a local hydrogel depot creates an inflammatory niche that boosts CAR-T proliferation and cures a solid tumor where systemic CAR-T fails.

## Design & Model

A polymer-nanoparticle (PNP) hydrogel (HPMC-C12 + PEG-PLA nanoparticles) co-delivering 2 million B7H3 CAR-T cells + 0.25 µg IL-15 per dose. Model: subcutaneous MED8A medulloblastoma in NSG mice.

## Quantitative Results

- Release kinetics: >80% of entrapped IL-15 retained at 1 week; hydrogel degradation half-life 8.9 ± 2.6 days.
- **Efficacy: PNP hydrogel + IL-15 cured 100% of mice by day 12**; hydrogel alone 70%. Bolus comparators: subcutaneous bolus 10%, intravenous bolus 40%, subcutaneous bolus + IL-15 40%.
- Distal (tumor-remote) administration still cured 100% by day 60.
- **>100-fold CAR-T expansion** versus IV control (P=0.0417).

## Limitations

Preclinical (mouse); single tumor model; the depot is a delivery adjunct, not a standalone therapy.

## Conclusion

A transient injectable hydrogel niche creates a local inflammatory environment that dramatically boosts CAR-T proliferation and tumor-reactive phenotype — curing 100% of mice versus 10–40% for bolus in a solid tumor, the setting where systemic CAR-T typically fails. It is the clearest preclinical case that local sustained co-delivery of cells + cytokine converts a pharmacokinetics-limited problem into a curative local one.
