# Rurik/Epstein 2022 — In-Vivo CAR-T from a Targeted mRNA-LNP

The landmark demonstrating that a targeting-ligand-decorated LNP can reprogram endogenous T cells into functional CAR-T in vivo from one injection — the LNP counterpart to lentiviral in-vivo CAR-T.

## Design & Model

An anti-CD5-conjugated LNP carrying 1-methylpseudouridine-modified mRNA encoding a FAP CAR (targeting fibroblast activation protein on activated cardiac fibroblasts). Transient, non-integrating. Model: C57BL/6 with angiotensin-II/phenylephrine cardiac-injury; echo n=7–8/condition, histology n=8–12.

## Quantitative Results

- In vitro: **83% of T cells FAPCAR⁺** with anti-CD5/LNP versus ~7% with non-targeted IgG/LNP.
- In vivo (spleen, 48 h post-injection): **17.5–24.7% FAPCAR⁺ T cells**; minimal in isotype/non-targeting controls.
- Dose: 10 µg LNP IV for cardiac studies.
- Efficacy: 5 of 12 treated animals had interstitial fibrosis indistinguishable from uninjured controls; left-ventricular volumes and diastolic function normalized.
- Specificity: IgG/LNP-FAPCAR and anti-CD5/LNP-GFP did not alter cardiac function or generate CAR-T.

## Limitations

Mouse-only; transient effect (mRNA); binary efficacy across small cohorts; anti-CD5 hits all T cells (not tumor-selective).

## Conclusion

A single injection of a targeted mRNA-LNP reprograms endogenous T cells into functional CAR-T in vivo — the proof of principle for off-the-shelf in-vivo CAR-T via LNP, and the direct analogue of the anti-CD8 DARPin-LNP approach being pursued against cancer. The transient, non-integrating mRNA makes the resulting CAR-T self-limiting, a safety feature.
