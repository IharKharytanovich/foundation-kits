# Wang/Cui 2020 — STING-Agonist Supramolecular Hydrogel

The clearest preclinical proof that local sustained release of an immunostimulant produces cure plus a systemic abscopal effect while eliminating systemic toxicity.

## Design & Model

A diCPT–iRGD peptide–drug conjugate self-assembles into nanotubes forming a supramolecular hydrogel, co-loading cyclic di-AMP (a STING agonist, 20 µg) + camptothecin (100 µg) per mouse. Models: intracranial GL-261 glioma, CT26 colon, 4T1 breast.

## Quantitative Results

- Release kinetics: cyclic di-AMP 98% released over 18 days; camptothecin 37.2% over 30 days; the depot was detectable in vivo beyond 35 days.
- **Efficacy:** intracranial GL-261 ~100% regression, 10/10 mice survived; CT26 ~88% regression; poorly-immunogenic 4T1 50% complete regression.
- **Abscopal / systemic immunity:** durable CD8 memory (24.4% central-memory, 11.7% effector-memory); **4T1 lung metastases eliminated** (no nodules versus abundant in saline); protective on rechallenge.
- **Local versus systemic:** localized release eliminated systemic toxicity while preserving efficacy.

## Limitations

Preclinical (mouse); no clinical translation of this construct yet.

## Conclusion

Extended intratumoral release of a STING agonist plus a chemotherapeutic from a self-assembled hydrogel cures intracranial glioma, generates durable systemic (abscopal) immunity against distant metastases, and eliminates the systemic toxicity that caps systemic STING dosing — the archetype for the hydrogel-depot "in-situ vaccine" logic, and the immunotherapy counterpart to the CAR-T niche hydrogel.
