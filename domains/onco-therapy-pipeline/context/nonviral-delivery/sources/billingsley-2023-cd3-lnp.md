# Billingsley 2023 — Ionizable Anti-CD3 LNP for In-Vivo CAR-T

Demonstrates a fully ionizable (clinically tractable) targeted LNP platform for in-vivo CAR-T and quantifies the effect of pan-T marker choice.

## Design & Model

A B10 ionizable LNP (C14-4 lipid) modified with maleimide-PEG and conjugated to anti-CD3 or anti-CD7 (pan-T markers), delivering CD19-CAR mRNA. C14-4 LNPs are already biased to spleen over liver, giving antibody conjugation a physicochemical head start. Mouse, n=4, doses 0.5 and 2 mg/kg.

## Quantitative Results

- **CAR⁺ T cells at 12 h (blood):** anti-CD3-LNP 15% (low dose) / 17% (high dose); anti-CD7-LNP 5% / 6%; untargeted B10 = 2%.
- Transient: only high-dose anti-CD3-LNP retained ~7% CAR⁺ at 36 h; none significant by 60 h.
- GFP reporter: anti-CD3-LNP reached 6.5% blood / 11% at 24 h, versus ~4% for a CD3-targeted MC3 clinical-lipid LNP — the C14-4 platform outperformed MC3.
- **Functional B-cell depletion (anti-CD19 CAR):** at 36 h, anti-CD3-LNP depleted circulating B cells 58% (low) / 90% (high); anti-CD7-LNP 30% / 47%.

## Limitations

Transfection modest (single-digit-to-teens %); short-lived; anti-CD3/CD7 are pan-T; the authors note the need for more robust therapeutic efficacy.

## Conclusion

A fully ionizable, clinically tractable targeted LNP can generate CAR-T in vivo and drive 90% B-cell depletion, with CD3 clearly outperforming CD7 and the spleen-biased C14-4 lipid beating the clinical MC3 lipid — quantitative guidance for the marker and lipid choices in LNP-based in-vivo cell engineering.
