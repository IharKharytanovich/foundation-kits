# Focused Ultrasound + Microbubbles — Opening the BBB

[[focused-ultrasound-delivery]] attacks the delivery wall from physics: it transiently opens the blood-brain barrier (BBB) so a systemically dosed drug can extravasate into a brain tumor, converting a barrier problem into a local-concentration problem. It is a physical form of [[delivery-bypass]] and the one platform in this cluster with mature human data.

## The Mechanism

Low-intensity pulsed ultrasound focused on brain tissue, combined with intravenously injected gas microbubbles, causes stable cavitation that mechanically loosens the tight junctions of the neurovascular unit. The opening is transient (closes within ~24–48 hours) and reversible. Two device classes exist: transcranial MR-guided systems (external helmet) and implantable emitters (SonoCloud, placed in the skull at surgery).

## The Quantitative Gains (validated in patients)

Across ~60 patients in phase 0/1 trials, focused ultrasound + microbubbles raised brain drug concentration substantially: paclitaxel 3.7-fold, carboplatin 5.9-fold, temozolomide 7.7-fold, and — critically for immunotherapy — the antibody trastuzumab by ~450% (a proof that even large molecules can be delivered). The implantable nine-emitter device produced a median BBB-opening volume of ~11.9 mL and ~90% disruption over the tumor cavity, with sessions of ~4.5 minutes repeatable across dozens of cycles. Safety has been manageable (transient headache, reversible encephalopathy at the highest doses).

## From PK Gain to Survival

The pharmacokinetic gain is now being tested for a survival benefit: SONOBIRD (a randomized phase 3 of the implantable device plus carboplatin versus standard of care in recurrent glioblastoma) is the first phase-3 test of whether the measured concentration increase translates into overall survival. A newly-diagnosed-glioblastoma survival signal has been reported at conference/press level but should be confirmed against the primary publication.

## Significance

Focused ultrasound is the strongest-evidenced physical bypass of the delivery wall, and uniquely addresses the blood-brain barrier — the one delivery problem local injection cannot solve for deep or diffuse brain tumors. It complements, rather than competes with, the biological and formulation strategies in this cluster.
